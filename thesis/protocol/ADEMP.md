## 1. Aims

- **Aim 1 — Fidelity.** How close are the cell-type mean profiles μ (genes × cell types) of each expression source to real **held-out** profiles of the same tissue and cell type?
- **Aim 2 — Benchmark sensitivity.** Do the error and the ranking of deconvolution methods change with the expression source? Which source gives the ranking closest to the one obtained on pseudo-spots built from **real held-out cells** (the real anchor)?

### Hypotheses
- **H1 (negative control).** Among the arms whose genes are the real genes (E, E′, C−, and G if it enters), C− has the lowest fidelity in every tissue, and its method ranking differs from the anchor. P is left out of the gene-by-gene comparison: its genes do not correspond to real genes, so its correlation with the held-out is about zero by construction, and in Aim 1 P is read only on distribution properties (5.1) (DECISIONS 2026-11-04, Luca).
- **H2 (donor-to-donor reference).** Arm E′ shows how close a second real donor gets to the held-out donor, in fidelity and in ranking agreement with the anchor. It gives the scale on which the other arms are read: a source close to E′ is "as good as another real donor". It is not a ceiling. A synthetic arm may reach or exceed E′ without anything being wrong: G (trained on public archives) is estimated from many donors, so it gives a population-average profile, which can be closer to a new donor than one other donor is. Exceeding E′ is therefore not evidence of leakage. The ceiling of Aim 1 is the split-half noise ceiling of the held-out donor (5.1); leakage is checked on the provenance of the data (section 9). *(DECISIONS 2026-10-26: E′ as donor-to-donor reference, Pierangela, approved by Luca; noise ceiling and provenance-based leakage check, Luca.)*
- **H3 (main question).** The method ranking of P or of G differs from the anchor more than the rankings of the reference arms O and E′ do (DECISIONS 2026-11-04, Luca). O separates the two causes of a difference from the anchor: anchor vs O is the effect of the noise model, O vs the other arms the effect of the source of μ. Decision rule in 5.4.
- "Differs" and "closest" are turned into decisions in section 5.4.

Declared caveat: OSCA notes that p-values between groups defined on the same data are valid "only for ranking", and scran calls the FDR "a rough measure of significance"; the author labels were derived from clustering of the same dataset. Fold-change of a marker = μ_E of its type / mean μ_E of the other types (as in 5.1). In each replicate a new μ^P: base means as in Splatter `splatSimGeneMeans` (gamma, outliers = median × log-normal factor); each type gets as many markers as found for it in training, drawn at random among the genes, each multiplied by a fold-change drawn with replacement from the empirical fold-changes of the training markers of the same type (per type, so each type keeps the strength of its own markers, e.g. Plasma; Pierangela, 2026-11-05) (as they are, including the few below 1 that `"some"` allows); a gene drawn for two types gets both fold-changes, and the sharing of markers between related types is not reproduced beyond chance (declared); columns normalised to 1. - **P as a coherent de novo world** (DECISIONS 2026-11-04, Luca). The genes of P do not correspond to real genes, so with the real training reference the spots of P would resemble no type and P would be a second C−, with H3 trivially true. The method reference of P is therefore simulated from μ^P in each replicate, together with μ^P: one simulated cell for each cell of the real (capped) reference of §4, with its cell type, its donor label (MuSiC needs one) and its library size, and counts from the common noise model (§2.3) with mean μ^P_gk × L. Same noise model and same number of cells per type as the real reference; P then asks whether a de novo benchmark gives the same ranking as the real one. Implementation (2026-11-05, `R/arm_p.R`, pre-registered in its header; test `tests/test_arm_p_o.R`): P genes are named `P00001…` (they are not real genes), as many as the genes of the reference; the library size of a simulated reference cell is the total count of its real counterpart over the kept genes, because the saved reference holds only those genes (in colon they carry 99.7–99.95% of the counts, the column sums of μ_E: declared); the NNLS profiles of P are the mean counts per simulated reference cell of each type, since P has no other cells (the real arms use all training cells: declared); in a replicate μ^P, φ^P, the reference and the spots of P are drawn in this order.
- **Dispersion of the P genes** (Pierangela, 2026-11-04). φ_g is fitted per real gene (§2.3), and a P gene has no real counterpart. Each P gene takes the φ of the real gene with the same rank of mean expression: P genes ranked by their mean over the types of μ^P (base means, before the markers), real genes by their pooled training mean, same number of genes, ties at random. Why: overdispersion depends on the mean (in Splatter the biological coefficient of variation of a gene is `bcv.common + 1/sqrt(mean)` times a chi-square scatter, `splatSimBCVMeans`, splatter source), so matching by rank keeps both the mean–dispersion relation and the real scatter of φ between genes of similar mean, without a new model.
- **Arm M removed** (DECISIONS 2026-11-04, Luca). In Aim 1 its profile equals E by construction; in Aim 2 it changed μ and noise together (secondary analysis since DECISIONS 2026-11-03), the cost of scDesign3 on brain was unknown and the calendar is a week late. Arm O takes its place in the computation; the comparison of noise models moves to the main simulator (Step 2). Its earlier rules (DECISIONS 2026-11-02 and 2026-11-03) and `scripts/04m_fit_scdesign3.R` are kept in the history and not used.
- C− permutation: one new permutation per replicate, so the result does not depend on one particular shuffle (DECISIONS 2026-10-30, Luca).
- G is evaluated in all three tissues. Criterion (DECISIONS 2026-10-30, Luca, replacing the unapproved 2026-10-27 row): GEM-1-sc was trained on the whole CELLxGENE of July 2024 (preprint, doi:10.1101/2025.09.08.674753, Methods), so a dataset is usable for G if it is **not in the CELLxGENE Census of 2024-07-01**. - Composition (Pierangela, 2026-10-31): π_s ~ Dirichlet(α) over the K spot types with α_k = c · p_k, where p_k is the share of type k among the training cells of the spot types and c is the concentration (next item; c = K until 2026-11-06); then the cells of the spot are drawn multinomially and the truth is the cell fraction. With c = K and equal shares this reduces to the symmetric α = 1 of the Open Problems spatial-decomposition simulator (GitHub `openproblems-bio/task_spatial_decomposition`, `dataset_simulator`, which accepts a vector α); synthspot's "real" dataset types likewise draw cell types with their real frequencies. Why not α = 1: with α = 1 a rare type gets as many cells as a common one, so the anchor would reuse the few held-out cells of rare types many times; proportions from training (not from the held-out donor, which must not shape its own test) make spots resemble the tissue and even out the reuse. Consequence, declared: rare types appear rarely, so methods are tested less on them.
- Concentration (Luca, DECISIONS 2026-11-06; replaces c = K of 2026-10-31 and the proposal of 2026-11-05): c per tissue and cells-per-spot level, with one rule in every tissue and at both levels, the median over the ROIs of Luca's R6 (main project) of c estimated from groups of 2 or 10 neighbouring real Visium HD nuclei (`protocol/R6_dirichlet_concentration_by_roi.csv`, rows `labels = corrected(ph1)`, the corrected labelling criterion PH-1 adopted by DEC-013 of the main project). Per ROI, c is the value at which the Dirichlet-multinomial variance of the neighbourhood compositions, S_multinom × (n + c)/(1 + c), equals the observed variance S_real (checked on the table, e.g. colon tumour ROI r1 at 10 nuclei: 0.258/0.0429 = 6.02, c = (10 − 6.02)/(6.02 − 1) = 0.79). Nuclei without a type are excluded (lymph node K = 6 types and about 14,000 nuclei per ROI, the typed 53% of the nuclei). Median, not mean, because in the brain at 10 nuclei one ROI has c = 110 and alone pulls the mean to 34. ROIs per tissue: colon = tumour and stromal regions of the same 10x Human Colon Cancer section (R6 archetypes A2 and A3, 10 ROIs), taken together because a Pelka tumour sample contains both tumour and stroma; lymphoid = 10x Human Lymph Node (A4, 5 ROIs); brain = 10x Mouse Brain (A5, 5 ROIs). Values at 2 and 10 cells: **colon 0.68 and 1.05, lymphoid 1.79 and 2.05, brain 3.37 and 21.8**. In all 20 ROIs c at 2 nuclei is below c at 10, because two neighbouring nuclei are more often of the same type than ten. Limits in §11. c comes from real data; the pilot checks its consequences (null baseline against its Dirichlet-multinomial formula, n_sim) and is not used to choose it. The colon pilot of 2026-11-05 used c = 1 at both levels (close to 1.05 at 10 cells, above 0.68 at 2 cells); the lymphoid and brain pilots use the values above.

### 2.3 Generating one replicate
1. For each spot, draw a composition from a Dirichlet(α) and sample the cells.
2. For each cell of type k, the count of gene g is negative binomial with mean μ_gk (scaled by library size) and dispersion φ_g estimated from the real training data. **The noise model is the same for all arms** (arm M, which had its own noise, was removed: DECISIONS 2026-11-04); only μ changes, and in P also the method reference (§2.1).
   Estimation (DECISIONS 2026-10-29; donor covariate in φ by Luca, 2026-10-30; sources in `notes/noise_model_digest.md`):
   - μ_gk = relative mean profile of type k on the training split, pooled: sum of the counts of gene g over the cells of type k / sum of all their counts (each column sums to 1 over all genes, just under 1 on the §2.6 kept genes, so that μ_gk × L matches the real counts). This is the maximum-likelihood rate of a Poisson model with log library size as offset. Same "profile × library size" structure as RCTD (Cable et al. 2022, doi:10.1038/s41587-021-00830-w: "average normalized expression of gene j within all cells of cell type k", counts ~ Poisson(N_i λ)) and muscat (Crowell et al. 2020, doi:10.1038/s41467-020-19894-4: mean = relative abundance × library size). RCTD averages per-cell ratios instead; the pooled version weights cells by library size and matches our own noise model.
   - Library size L of a simulated cell: drawn at random from the real library sizes of training cells of the same type; mean count = μ_gk × L. Empirical, as in muscat (simulated cells take the library size of reference cells of their group), rather than parametric (log-normal in Splatter, Zappia et al. 2017, doi:10.1186/s13059-017-1305-0).
   - φ_g: one per gene, fitted on the training split with `glmGamPoi` (design `~ cell_type + donor`, L as offset; the donor covariate keeps donor-to-donor variability out of φ, otherwise the simulated arms would be noisier than the anchor, which comes from one donor: DECISIONS 2026-10-30, Luca; Ahlmann-Eltze & Huber 2020, doi:10.1093/bioinformatics/btaa1009: better overdispersion estimates "on datasets with many small counts", no dataset size limit), on a sample of 50,000 training cells in every tissue (20,000 until the check of 2026-11-11, below), stratified by cell type (proportional to each type's share) with a fixed seed: one rule for all tissues, because all brain cells (~900,000) do not fit in memory; `glm_gp()` itself offers subsampling (`subsample`). Pre-registered check in colon, where all training cells fit: φ from the sample vs φ from all cells; the sample is adequate if the correlation of log φ across genes is ≥ 0.99 and the median ratio φ_sample/φ_all is between 0.95 and 1.05, otherwise the sample rises to 50,000 cells in every tissue (DECISIONS 2026-10-29, refined 2026-10-30; `tests/test_phi_sample.R`). Outcome (DECISIONS 2026-11-11; run on 2026-11-11, after the pilots, although it was due before them): colon pilot reference, 20,001 sampled vs 61,134 cells, 16,602 genes; correlation of log φ 0.882 (FAIL), median ratio 1.025 (PASS), 1,060 genes with a ratio outside 0.5–2, so the sample is unbiased but the φ of single genes is imprecise; the sample rises to 50,000 cells in every tissue (same stratified sampling, seed 20261031) and the pilot references are rebuilt. In colon 50,000 cells are 82% of all, so a repeated check would pass almost by construction and is not a condition. n_sim comes from the colon pilot, so only that pilot is rerun with the new φ and n_sim is recomputed by the rule of §6. Fit details: raw per-gene maximum-likelihood φ (`overdispersion_shrinkage = FALSE`, glmGamPoi default Cox–Reid adjustment kept), genes fitted in parallel chunks (identical to one single fit, checked 2026-10-31). Cost grows with the square of the number of coefficients: brain (27 types + 197 donors) needed about half a day on the server with 20,000 cells. Per-gene NB dispersion as in muscat (edgeR `estimateDisp`) and in cell2location's model (Kleshchevnikov et al. 2022, doi:10.1038/s41587-021-01139-4: "gene- and batch-specific over-dispersion").
3. Sum the cells of the spot.

**Real anchor:** pseudo-spots built by summing **real cells** of the held-out donor, with the same Dirichlet compositions. The truth is known because each cell's type is known.
- Every anchor pseudo-spot is built inside a single sample, because a real spot never mixes cells from two samples (DECISIONS 2026-10-26). Colon uses MMRp tumour samples only and lymphoid the thoracic lymph node only (DECISIONS 2026-10-26 "Dataset", refined 2026-10-27, approved by Luca 2026-10-30), so there is one μ per tissue.
- Depth of the anchor: its spots are thinned at random to the same target depth as the simulated arms (DECISIONS 2026-10-30, Luca).

### 2.4 Common random numbers
This follows from PROTOCOLLO §5.2 (only the source of μ changes; the anchor uses "la stessa composizione Dirichlet"). Within replicate i, all arms and the anchor use the **same** compositions and the same cell-type draws per spot; only μ (and therefore the counts) differs. Differences between arms are then not inflated by different compositions, which lowers the Monte Carlo SE of between-arm comparisons (Morris §5.3). All methods are run on the same datasets (Morris recommendation).
- **Implementation** (2026-11-05, `scripts/04_simulate.R`, pre-registered in its header). A replicate is, per tissue, the anchor and the arms E, C−, E′, O, P at the 4 conditions (24 datasets; 72 over the three tissues). One μ^P, one P reference and one C− permutation per replicate and tissue, shared by its four conditions, so the P reference is trained once per tissue and replicate (§6). The two depth levels thin the same raw spots, so the depth comparison is also paired. With one depth level per tissue (DECISIONS 2026-11-12) a replicate has 2 conditions per tissue (12 datasets; 36 over the three tissues) and there is no depth comparison; `04_simulate.R` still has the levels 175 and 4,900 and is updated to the HD level of each tissue before the main run (the colon test below was run with the two levels). Every random step has its own L'Ecuyer-CMRG sub-stream of the replicate stream (§8), so adding G or cutting an arm leaves every other dataset unchanged; tissues use disjoint streams. The P reference is not stored: μ^P, φ^P and the RNG state of its sub-stream are saved and `reference_p_from_world()` (`R/arm_p.R`) rebuilds it exactly. Colon test on the pilot reference (2026-11-05): all checks PASS (same replicate twice identical; without P and E′ the other datasets identical; P reference rebuilt identical; replicates differ); per-spot correlation with the anchor O 0.878 > E 0.851 > E′ 0.846 > C− −0.003; observed reuse of held-out cells matches 500 × cells × p_k / n_k; 210 s and 70 MB per replicate and tissue on the PC, plus 55 s to rebuild the P reference. The main run is blocked in the script until this ADEMP is approved.

### 2.6 Filtering of the real data (before building μ)
PROTOCOLLO §5.2 does not mention this step; the filter below is fixed (Pierangela, 2026-11-03) and already applied (cells in `02_split.R`, genes in `03_reference.R`). The same minimal filter is applied to every real dataset before μ, φ_g, the reference for the methods and the anchor are computed. It follows Crowell et al. 2023 (Additional file 1, §6.1.2), who filtered their real references "as insufficient numbers of cells and low-quality observations can interfere with estimation of simulation parameters". Filtered data are saved in `data/derived/`; the downloaded files are never changed (PROTOCOLLO §3.7).
1. **Cell types:** remove a cell type from a split (training, held-out donor, E′ donor) if it has fewer than 50 cells there. In Crowell the groups are batches or clusters; for us they are cell types. The cell types removed are listed in the report. Why the training split must contain every type that appears in the spots: the reference must cover every cell type present in the mixtures, otherwise deconvolution error rises (in Avila Cobos et al. 2020, Nat Commun 11:5650, doi:10.1038/s41467-020-19015-1, removing one cell type from the reference increased the RMSE at least 2-fold in several cases); in machine-learning terms, training and test must share the same set of classes, a test class unseen in training being a form of dataset shift (Moreno-Torres et al. 2012, Pattern Recognition 45:521–530, doi:10.1016/j.patcog.2011.06.019). The reverse (reference types absent from the spots) is allowed and scored as false positives.
   Consequences (DECISIONS 2026-10-29, approved by Luca 2026-10-30): sources E, P, G, C− and the method reference use every type with ≥50 training cells (brain 27, colon 18, lymphoid 30; `type_kept` in `<tissue>_types.csv`). Aim 1 scores only the types with ≥50 held-out cells (20 / 8 / 9); the others are "not evaluated", not penalised. Pseudo-spots of every arm and the anchor use the types with ≥50 cells in training, held-out and E′ (brain 11, colon 6, lymphoid 7), because E′ has no profile for some held-out types (9 / 2 / 2): every arm, E′ included, then gets the same compositions (§2.4). The method reference keeps all training types, so reference types absent from the spots have true proportion 0 and proportion given to them counts as error (false positive). E′ has 11 / 7 / 13 types; in Aim 1 it is compared with the held-out on the types with ≥50 cells in both (11 / 6 / 7).
2. **Genes:** keep genes with a count greater than 1 in at least 10 cells. The gene list is decided on the training split only and the same list is used for every split and arm, so that all μ matrices have the same genes and the held-out data do not influence the choice.
3. **Cells:** keep cells with at least 100 detected genes (non-zero counts).

## 3. Estimands

- **Primary (Aim 2):** the proportion π_sk of each cell type k in each spot s, as a share of **cells** (PROTOCOLLO §5.3), the same truth for every method. It is a vector per spot, not a single scalar θ as in Morris; we score it with RMSE and Jensen–Shannon divergence (section 5).
- **Scale of the method outputs** (DECISIONS 2026-11-03, Pierangela): with the normalisation recommended by the authors (section 4), RCTD and spatialDWLS estimate the share of **UMI** of each type, not of cells: RCTD by definition of its weights ("the proportion of the contribution of cell type k to pixel i", Cable et al. 2022, Methods; `normalize_weights` only rescales to 1), spatialDWLS because its signature is built on per-cell normalised expression and the solution is only rescaled to 1 (spatialDWLS 0.1.1 documentation and source). MuSiC (weighted proportions) and NNLS (profiles = mean counts per cell) estimate shares of cells. The gap between UMI and cell shares is reported as a property of a method read as cell proportions; it depends only on the library sizes of the cells, which are the same in every arm, so it does not bias the comparison between sources of μ (on the anchor the cells are real and the gap can differ slightly). Colon pilot: RCTD overestimates EpiT (median library size of the pilot training cells 12,453 UMI vs 2,146–2,298 in TCD8/TCD4; `colon_pilot.rds`, `L_by_type`) by 0.24–0.28 on the anchor at 10 cells.
- **UMI-share truth** (sensitivity, section 7.2): per spot, the share of the spot's UMI from each type = sum of the library sizes of its cells / sum over all its cells (`umi_truth()`, `R/simulate_spots.R`).
- **Ranking target (Aim 2):** the ordering of the methods by mean error within each arm × condition, compared with the ordering on the anchor.
- **Secondary (Aim 1):** the cell-type profiles μ and their markers, compared with the held-out real profiles.

## 5. Performance measures

### 5.1 Aim 1 — fidelity (per arm × tissue × cell type)
- Correlation of log-means between source and held-out (PROTOCOLLO §5.5). Gene list: the §2.6 list chosen on the training split. Pearson correlation on the log-profiles, Spearman as sensitivity (DECISIONS 2026-10-30, Luca); log-profile = log(1 + 10⁴ μ), i.e. counts per 10,000 with pseudocount 1 (Pierangela, 2026-11-03): the `LogNormalize` default of `Seurat::NormalizeData` (`scale.factor = 10000`, counts divided by the cell total, then `log1p`; Seurat documentation, checked 2026-11-04), the same scale used by the spatialDWLS wrapper. Computed on each of the 4 donors of the held-out group (§2.5); E′ on all 6 pairs of the group.
- **Noise ceiling** (DECISIONS 2026-10-26): for each tissue and cell type, the cells of the held-out donor are split at random into two halves of equal size within each cell type (n_k/2 cells each; if n_k is odd, the extra cell goes to one of the two halves at random), so that Spearman–Brown's equal-halves assumption holds; the correlation r between the two half-profiles (same correlation, same genes as above) is corrected with Spearman–Brown, reliability = 2r / (1 + r); √reliability is the highest correlation any source can reach with the held-out. Repeated over B random splits, averaged (Pierangela, 2026-11-03): B is the smallest number of splits for which the Monte Carlo SE of the averaged ceiling, SD(ρ_max)/√B, is below 0.005 for every cell type (below the second decimal reported); SD = upper 95% limit (chi-square) from a pilot of 10 splits, B = ⌈(SD_upper / 0.005)²⌉, at least 10, computed in each tissue with the same rule (DECISIONS 2026-11-11); seed 42. B per tissue: `[VALUE]`. Cell types with few held-out cells get a lower ceiling; fidelity is read against it.
- **Overlap of the top-k markers** (Pierangela, 2026-11-03): per type, the k genes with the highest log2 fold-change vs the mean of the other types, on the log(1 + 10⁴ μ) scale of the correlation, in the source and in the held-out profiles; overlap = Jaccard index. Main k = 100; sensitivity = Jaccard curve for k = 10, 20, 50, 100, 200. Why: no established value. Spotless runs marker-based methods with the top 100 markers per type (Sang-aram et al. 2024, doi:10.7554/eLife.88431, Methods); scDesign3 selected the top 50 per type (Song et al. 2024); in a benchmark of 59 marker-selection methods, n = 5, 10, 20, 40 "had little effect on the overall ranking" (Pullin & McCarthy 2024, Genome Biol, doi:10.1186/s13059-024-03183-0); GEM-1 reports the Jaccard index over a range of top-k values (Koytiger et al. 2025, Fig. 2G). 100 also matches the spatialDWLS signature (section 4).
- **Cell-type separability** (Pierangela, 2026-11-03): silhouette width (Rousseeuw 1987, J Comput Appl Math 20:53–65) with cell type as label, on Euclidean distances in PCA space, as in Crowell et al. 2023 (doi:10.1186/s13059-023-02904-1; Additional file 1, Table S3: "cluster's silhouette function on Euclidean distances in PCA space"). Cells generated from each source with the common noise model, as many per type as in the held-out donor, seed 42, vs the real held-out cells; mean silhouette per type. Settings (Pierangela, 2026-11-04): log-normalised counts (`scuttle::logNormCounts`); 2,000 highly variable genes by Seurat VST (`FindVariableFeatures`, `selection.method = "vst"`, `nfeatures = 2000`, its defaults) on the raw training counts, chosen once and used for every arm and the held-out; first 50 principal components (`scater::calculatePCA` default, as in Crowell). Why: Seurat-VST had the highest overall ranking among the feature-selection methods of Zappia et al. 2025 (Nat Methods, doi:10.1038/s41592-025-02624-3), where scran/OSCA was not among the top, and 2,000 features "consistently produced high scores"; 2,000 lies within 1,000–5,000 (Luecken & Theis 2019, Mol Syst Biol 15:e8746, doi:10.15252/msb.20188746). Log1p normalisation kept: it "performs as well or better than the more sophisticated alternatives" (Ahlmann-Eltze & Huber 2023, Nat Methods, doi:10.1038/s41592-023-01814-1). Crowell used scran with 500 genes: declared difference.
- **Arm P in Aim 1** (DECISIONS 2026-11-04, Luca): its gene-by-gene correlation and marker overlap with the held-out are about zero by construction (its genes are not the real genes), so P is read only on distribution properties, each against the held-out and against E: separability (silhouette, as above); distribution of the gene means (quantiles 10, 50, 90, 99, 99.9% of the log(1 + 10⁴ μ) values over genes, and the two-sample Kolmogorov–Smirnov distance); distribution of the fold-changes of the top-100 markers per type (same quantiles and distance) (measures: Pierangela, 2026-11-04).
- Monte Carlo SE only where μ is random: P and C−, and G if repeated queries return different profiles; E and E′ are fixed given the split (Pierangela, 2026-11-03). Number of draws of C− and P (DECISIONS 2026-11-11, Pierangela): the rule of the noise ceiling, i.e. a pilot of 10 draws per tissue, SD_upper = upper 95% limit (chi-square, as for n_sim) of the SD of each measure per cell type and donor, n = ⌈(SD_upper / 0.005)²⌉, the largest over types and measures, at least 10 (the pilot draws are kept); computed in each tissue (Morris et al. 2019, §5.3).

### 5.2 Aim 2 — error at two levels (Morris §5.2, adapted)
- **Inside replicate i:** RMSE_i = √(mean over spots × cell types of (π̂_sk − π_sk)²); JSD_i = mean over spots of the Jensen–Shannon divergence between π̂_s and π_s.
- **Across replicates:** mean RMSE over n_sim replicates, with MCSE = SD(RMSE_i) / √n_sim; same for JSD.
- Per method × arm × condition, and on the anchor.
- **RMSE relative to the null baseline** (DECISIONS 2026-11-04, Luca): mean RMSE of the method / mean RMSE of the null baseline (7.2 item 3) in the same arm × condition; below 1 the method adds information beyond the expected composition. Reported next to every RMSE, with the paired difference RMSE_i(method) − RMSE_i(null) within replicate and its MCSE (SD/√n_sim), so that a ratio near 1 is read against its Monte Carlo error; on the UMI-share truth as sensitivity (`scripts/06b_pilot_nsim.R`, Tables 8–8c; ratio of means, not the mean of per-replicate ratios, which in the colon pilot differ by at most 0.0016).
- Secondary: failure rate per method × arm × condition, with MCSE (7.2).
- This per-replicate RMSE over a vector of proportions is our adaptation; Morris defines MSE for one scalar θ.

### 5.3 Aim 2 — rankings
- Rank the methods by mean RMSE (primary) and by mean JSD (secondary) within each arm × condition.
- **Kendall τ** between each arm's ranking and the anchor ranking.
- **Kendall W** across arms (agreement of all arms).
- MCSE of τ and W: bootstrap over replicates, **B = 1,000**, seed 42 (Pierangela, 2026-11-03). Efron & Tibshirani 1993 (*An Introduction to the Bootstrap*, ch. 6 and 19): B = 200 is rarely exceeded for a standard error, about 1,000 for percentile intervals (rule reported by several secondary sources; page to check in the book before citing).
- With 5 methods there are only 10 pairs, so τ takes 11 values (−1 to 1 in steps of 0.2); a one-swap change is τ = 0.8. Report also the pairwise RMSE differences between methods with their MCSE, so a swap within Monte Carlo error is not read as a real change (rule in 5.4).

### 5.4 Decision rules
- **Sign change** of a method pair (DECISIONS 2026-10-30, made explicit 2026-11-04, Luca): the RMSE difference between the two methods has opposite signs in the arm and in the anchor, and both differences exceed 2 × their MCSE. A ranking **differs** from the anchor if it has at least one sign change in that condition.
- **H3** (DECISIONS 2026-11-04, Luca): count the sign changes against the anchor over method pairs × conditions × tissues, per arm. H3 holds for P (or G) if its count exceeds the counts of both O and E′. Declared: with 10 pairs × 2 conditions × 3 tissues (4 conditions until v1.4) some sign changes appear by chance; the counts of O and E′, which differ from the anchor only through the noise model and the donor, are the reference for this background.
- An arm is **closest** to the anchor, among the source arms E, P and G (O, E′ and C− are reference and control arms): highest Kendall τ with the anchor, ties broken by the mean |ΔRMSE| against the anchor (Pierangela, 2026-11-03).

## 6. Number of replicates (n_sim)

- Key measure: the paired difference in RMSE between two methods within the same replicate, D_i = RMSE_i(m1) − RMSE_i(m2) (DECISIONS 2026-11-03, Luca): differences decide the rankings and, under common random numbers, vary less than the RMSE of one method.
- Rule: 2 × MCSE(D) ≤ δ/2, with MCSE(D) = SD_upper(D) / √n_sim, so n_sim = (4 × SD_upper / δ)². SD_upper is the upper 95% confidence limit of SD(D_i) from the pilot (chi-square, 9 df: about 1.83 × SD with 10 replicates), because with 10 replicates the SD itself is uncertain by about 24% (DECISIONS 2026-10-30, Luca). δ = smallest relevant difference between two methods = **0.02** (DECISIONS 2026-11-03, Pierangela). Largest SD_upper over the three tissues, from the pilots with the c rule (2026-11-09 to 2026-11-11; `<tissue>_crule_pilot_nsim.csv`, `scripts/06b_pilot_nsim.R`): colon 0.065 (spatialDWLS − NNLS, arm C−, 10 cells, 4,900 UMI; n_sim 169, 73% of the method pairs differ by at least δ), lymphoid 0.019 (n_sim 14, 58%), brain 0.029 (n_sim 33, 69%), so n_sim = 169 (colon; DECISIONS 2026-11-03, precision of 2026-11-11; replaced by 118 on 2026-11-12, see the update below); the null baseline matches its formula in every tissue. ## 7. Checking the estimates: failures (Morris §5.1)

### 7.1 No extra filter on the pseudo-spots
PROTOCOLLO §5.2 has no quality filter on the pseudo-spots, and we add none: the real data are filtered upstream (2.6), and each method applies only its own built-in filter (section 4), as a user running the published pipeline would. Spots that a method drops or cannot handle are failures (7.2).
- Depth levels (2.2): the lowest spot target is 124 UMI (lymphoid, 2 cells × 62), close to the built-in threshold of RCTD (100 UMI per spot); with exact thinning every spot reaches its target, so RCTD discards none (declared, DECISIONS 2026-11-12). Until v1.4 the lowest target was 350 UMI (2 cells × 175). The general rule: check where the low level falls relative to the built-in threshold of RCTD (100 UMI per spot) and any threshold of spatialDWLS. If it is close, many spots would be dropped and the difference between methods would come from the filter rather than from the deconvolution. Checked (Pierangela, 2026-11-03): spatialDWLS 0.1.1 has no spot filter in its source (its only filter is the 1/n_cell rule on cell types per cluster, section 7.2); in the colon pilot RCTD dropped 6 spots in all (arm C−) of 60,000 spot-runs (500 spots × 10 replicates × 4 conditions × 3 datasets), so the low level is not driven by the filters. Pilots with the c rule (§7.2; 20,000 spots per dataset): RCTD failed on 24 colon spots (arm C−), no lymphoid spot and 1 brain spot (arm C−), so the same holds in every tissue.

### 7.2 Failures
1. **Count** failures per method × arm × condition (non-convergence, dropped spots, missing values, a proportion below −1e-5, or proportions whose sum differs from 1 by more than 1e-5; values between −1e-5 and 0 are numerical zeros, set to 0, and the proportions are then rescaled to 1 (Pierangela, 2026-11-03). Basis: in the 40 colon pilot files the sums after the wrappers deviate from 1 by at most 9e-16; RCTD returns 2 negative values out of 1,079,892 (2 of 59,994 spots), the largest −7.0e-7, solver residues, not estimates; no value lies between −1e-5 and −0.01, so any threshold from about 1e-6 to 1e-3 classifies the pilot identically; cell2location computes in float32 (relative precision about 1.2e-7, IEEE 754), so a sum over 30 types can deviate by about 3.6e-6. 1e-5 leaves a margin above both. Pre-registered check: the same residues are measured on the lymphoid and brain pilots and on cell2location; if any exceeds 1e-5, the tolerance is revised by amendment before any result `[VALUE]`) and report them as a result. Never drop them silently: failures cluster in the hardest datasets, so dropping them makes a method look better.
2. **No exclusion threshold** (DECISIONS 2026-11-03, Luca): the failure rate depends on the arm, so it is an outcome, not a filter; a threshold would give rankings with different methods in different arms, and τ would not be comparable. All five methods always stay in the ranking. See also Pawel et al. 2026 (doi:10.1080/00031305.2025.2540002): excluding methods hides failure-linked poor performance.
3. **Main analysis:** every failed spot gets the mean cell-type proportions, the best guess without information (null baseline, as in Spotless), so failure counts in the RMSE and spots and methods are the same in every arm. Proportions = share of each cell type among all training cells (full training split, not the reference capped at 1,000 cells per type), over all reference types; types absent from the spots count as false positives (DECISIONS 2026-10-29). The fill weighs on the RMSE only when the method, where it answers, beats this null baseline: colon pilot at 2 cells, fill ≈ 0.14–0.16 RMSE, RCTD better, NNLS and MuSiC similar, spatialDWLS worse in 5 of 6 arm × depth cells. So the null baseline (fill on every spot) is reported as a reference value, and the failure rate (item 4) is always read next to the RMSE. Why no method beats it at 10 cells on the anchor (colon pilot; DECISIONS 2026-11-04): with α_k = c · p_k the expected composition of every spot is the training share p_k whatever c is, on the anchor too, so the null baseline is almost an oracle for the RMSE; its RMSE is fixed by the spread of the true compositions around p, p_k(1 − p_k)[1/(c + 1) + c/((c + 1) n)] per type with n cells per spot (Dirichlet-multinomial moments, `scripts/06b_pilot_nsim.R`), which shrinks with n. Colon pilot with c = K (the rule before 2026-11-06), three routes agree: this formula 0.151 (2 cells) and 0.089 (10 cells), Monte Carlo 0.151 and 0.089, observed 0.151–0.152 and 0.089–0.090; the best constant guess (p over the spot types only) gives 0.1505 and 0.0884 (computed 2026-11-04 from `colon_pilot.rds` and `colon_pilot_rmse_methods.csv`). Pilots with the c rule (2026-11-09 to 2026-11-11; `nsim_<tissue>_crule.log`, `scripts/06b_pilot_nsim.R`), formula at 2 and 10 cells: colon 0.182 and 0.149, lymphoid 0.137 and 0.105, brain 0.134 and 0.066; the observed null RMSE agrees within 0.001 in every dataset and condition (pre-registered check |observed − formula| < 0.005: PASS). RMSE relative to the null baseline, anchor, colon pilot (per replicate, mean over 10; `colon_pilot_rmse_relative_null.csv`): at 2 cells RCTD 0.75–0.87, the other methods 1.04–1.30; at 10 cells all methods 1.18–1.98. The UMI-share explanation of 2026-11-03 holds for RCTD only: against the UMI-share truth, with the UMI-share null (sensitivity (c)), RCTD beats the null baseline at 10 cells on the anchor (0.48–0.64); NNLS and MuSiC estimate cell shares and lose on their own scale (1.21–1.60), so for them the near-oracle baseline is the explanation. A method that fails gets the null proportions, so failing lowers its RMSE: spatialDWLS at 2 cells on the anchor 1.30 on the spots it returns vs 1.27 with its 11% of failed spots filled (C−, 20–32% failed: 1.85–1.89 vs 1.66–1.72); the failure rate is therefore always reported.
4. **Secondary measure:** failure rate (failed spots / spots, per replicate) per method × arm × condition, averaged over replicates with MCSE = SD / √n_sim, compared with the anchor.
5. **Sensitivity analyses:** (a) spots returned by **all** methods in the replicate (common set; a method failing on every spot of a replicate is left out of the set instead of emptying it: DECISIONS 2026-11-02); (b) spatialDWLS with `n_cell = 50` (Giotto default); (c) each method scored against the truth its output represents: RCTD and spatialDWLS against the UMI-share truth, MuSiC and NNLS against the cell-share truth (section 3; DECISIONS 2026-11-03); n_sim is set on the main analysis, so (c) is less precise (colon pilot: SD_upper(D) 0.069 vs 0.045). Recompute RMSE, τ and W and report whether the ranking changes.
- spatialDWLS `n_cell` = true number of cells per spot, as the authors define it (DECISIONS 2026-11-03, Luca): its 1/n_cell filter is a property of the method (same rule in Giotto and the standalone package), so failure at 2 cells is reported as a result (Visium HD 8 µm bins hold 1–2 cells).
- Failures can still differ by arm through μ (e.g. C−). Colon pilot: only spatialDWLS fails, only at 2 cells per spot (E 3–15%, C− 20–32%, anchor 6–11% of spots; `n_cell = 2` makes its filter threshold 1/n_cell = 0.5 equal to the true proportion); RCTD 6 spots in C−. Pilots with the c rule (failed spots summed over 10 replicates × 4 conditions, 20,000 spots per dataset): colon spatialDWLS C− 2,329, E 458, RCTD C− 24; lymphoid only spatialDWLS, 2,069–3,235 per dataset (anchor included); brain spatialDWLS C− 5,703, E 891, anchor 840, RCTD C− 1; MuSiC and NNLS none, no errors. So RCTD also fails, rarely and only in C−.

## 9. Counter-proofs (PROTOCOLLO §5.6)

1. **C−** must give low fidelity and rankings different from the anchor. If rankings do not change even with meaningless profiles, the benchmark is insensitive to expression level: that is a result and is reported.
2. **E′** is the reference for donor-to-donor variability, not a ceiling: a synthetic source (P or G) above E′ is not evidence of leakage and is reported with its explanation (for G: a population-average profile, estimated from many donors, can be closer to a new donor than a single other donor is; P, built de novo, has no such explanation, so a P result above E′ is examined further). The ceiling is the noise ceiling of 5.1; a source above it beyond Monte Carlo error points to an error or leakage and is investigated before going on. *(Replaces PROTOCOLLO §5.6 point 2: DECISIONS 2026-10-26.)*
3. **Leakage, checked on the provenance of the data** (DECISIONS 2026-10-26), always and not only when a source looks too good: (a) automatic test that no donor ID appears in two splits (for colon, both samples of a donor in the same split); (b) for arm G, only datasets absent from the CELLxGENE Census of 2024-07-01 (GEM-1-sc training data; criterion and check in section 2.1, DECISIONS 2026-10-30); PsychAD, Pelka 2021 and Wells 2025 are all absent.
4. **Arm O** (PROTOCOLLO §5.6 point 4, DECISIONS 2026-11-04): if the ranking of O already differs from the anchor, part of the difference between the arms and the anchor comes from the noise model and not from the source of μ; the differences of P and G are read against those of O and E′ (5.4).

Purpose: provide the values the rules need (SD for n_sim, runtimes, failures, seed check, depth), not to look at results.

## 11. Declared limits (for the Discussion)
- Aim 1 uses a group of 4 randomly drawn held-out donors per tissue (distribution of donor-to-donor variability); Aim 2 uses one held-out donor (plus a second as sensitivity if time allows), so its rankings are conditional on that donor. Full internal-external cross-validation (each donor left out in turn; Steyerberg & Harrell 2016, doi:10.1016/j.jclinepi.2015.04.005; Debray et al. 2023, doi:10.1136/bmj-2022-071058; leave-one-subject-out in MuSiC, Wang et al. 2019) is not done for compute and time (DECISIONS 2026-10-29, 2026-10-30).
- The true number of cells per spot is given to the methods that accept it (oracle information, DECISIONS 2026-10-30): real data do not provide it.
- Lymphoid data are 10x 5′ (Wells 2025): this may disadvantage GEM-1 for technical reasons (DECISIONS 2026-10-26).
- Potential limit for G: the CELLxGENE version used to train the gem-1-sc served by the API is not known (DECISIONS 2026-10-30).
- Training sizes differ between tissues (brain 197 donors, colon 16, lymphoid 17), together with chemistry (nuclei 3′ v3, cells 3′ v2, cells 5′ v2): patterns compared between tissues confound tissue with training size and technology.
- One Visium HD depth level per tissue (DECISIONS 2026-11-12): conclusions on standard Visium spots are outside the thesis, and depth, different in each tissue (174, 62 and 121 UMI per cell), joins the factors confounded with tissue. Each depth comes from one or two 10x samples; the lymphoid value averages two chemistries (FFPE probes and fresh-frozen 3′); the brain sample is a shallowly sequenced FFPE brain tumour, while our brain data are nuclei of healthy cortex. In the ROIs of the main project used for c (R4, `docs/BIO_REFERENCES.md` B-281, B-291, B-303, B-316) the UMI per 8 µm bin are about 1.5 (colon, lymphoid) and 2.8 times (brain, mouse) higher than the whole-tissue means of 10x, because the ROIs are cell-rich regions; the brain depth is the most uncertain. The values of PROTOCOLLO 1.5 are kept (Pierangela, 2026-11-13).
- spatialDWLS is run through a new standalone re-implementation, not the Giotto version evaluated in earlier benchmarks.
- Pseudo-spots have no geometry (PROTOCOLLO §8), which is why CARD is not included.
- RCTD and spatialDWLS estimate UMI shares while the main truth is cell shares (section 3): part of their error is a difference of definition, not of estimation; the gap is the same in every arm, but it can make μ-driven changes in their RMSE look smaller, which sensitivity (c) of section 7.2 checks.
- The Dirichlet concentration c (§2.2) comes from Visium HD neighbourhoods typed with R6's catalogue (3–6 types per tissue), coarser than the authors' labels used here; in the lymph node 47% of the nuclei have no type and are excluded from c; the R6 brain is mouse cortex, while our brain data are human (DECISIONS 2026-11-06).
- The noise model is the same in every arm, so the study does not test model-based generators with their own noise (arm M removed, DECISIONS 2026-11-04); its effect is bounded by arm O (anchor vs O) and the comparison of noise models is left to the main simulator.
- Arm P changes the method reference as well as μ (DECISIONS 2026-11-04): its ranking answers whether a fully de novo benchmark agrees with the real one, not the effect of μ alone. Its genes do not correspond to real genes, so its Aim 1 fidelity is limited to distribution properties.
- The null baseline is close to an oracle under our composition model (7.2 item 3): RMSE alone can make methods look worse than an uninformed guess at 10 cells per spot, so RMSE is always read next to the RMSE relative to the null baseline.
