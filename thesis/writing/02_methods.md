This chapter describes the design of the simulation study through which the question raised in Chapter 1 is addressed. The study was planned according to the ADEMP framework of Morris et al. (2019), and the protocol, including its hypotheses, decision rules and number of replicates, was written and approved before any result was examined *[pending Luca's approval; date of approval to be added]*. The chapter first gives an overview of the design and then follows the order in which the study is carried out: the selection of the real datasets and the reasons for each choice, the partition of their donors and the filtering of their cells, the mechanisms that generate the simulated data, the deconvolution methods, the estimands and performance measures, the number of replicates and its justification in terms of Monte Carlo error, the treatment of failed runs, and finally the software environment that makes every result reproducible.

## 2.1 Design of the simulation study

The study is organised around the five components that Morris et al. (2019) recommend specifying in advance: aims, data-generating mechanisms, estimands, methods and performance measures. Its two aims correspond to the objectives stated in Section 1.7. The first aim concerns fidelity, namely how closely the matrix of mean expression values μ, with one row per gene and one column per cell type, produced by each expression source approximates the profiles of the same tissue and cell type measured in a real donor who did not contribute to its construction. The second aim concerns the sensitivity of the benchmark, namely whether the error and the ranking of deconvolution methods change with the expression source, and which source yields the ranking closest to the one obtained on pseudo-spots built from real cells of the held-out donor, the real anchor.

The design varies a single principal factor, the source of μ, across six arms, while every other component of the data-generating process is shared. This choice responds directly to the limitation identified in Section 1.5: in the comparison of Crowell et al. (2023) each simulator combined its own expression model with its own noise model, so that a change in the ranking of methods could not be attributed to any one component, whereas here the generator, the noise model, the spot compositions and the reference supplied to the methods are identical in every arm, and any difference between arms can be ascribed to the expression source alone. Three secondary factors, the tissue, the number of cells per spot and the sequencing depth, are crossed with the principal factor in a fixed grid, so that the conclusions can be examined in settings of different composition and difficulty.

Three hypotheses were stated before the study *[pending Luca's approval, together with the thresholds of Section 2.6.4]*. The first concerns the negative control: the arm with permuted gene labels is expected to show the lowest fidelity in every tissue and a ranking of methods that differs from that of the anchor. The second concerns the arm derived from a second real donor, which is expected to show how close an independent individual comes to the held-out donor, both in fidelity and in agreement with the anchor ranking, and therefore to provide the scale on which the other arms are read. The third is the principal hypothesis of the thesis: at least one synthetic arm yields a ranking of methods that differs from the ranking obtained on the anchor.

## 2.2 Data sources and their selection

### 2.2.1 Selection criteria

The real data serve three purposes: they provide the training data from which empirical and model-based profiles, dispersions and the reference for the methods are estimated; they provide the held-out donor against which fidelity is measured and from whose cells the anchor is built; and they provide the second independent donor of the corresponding control arm. All three roles are filled, within each tissue, by different donors of a single dataset (DECISIONS 2026-10-26, "Dataset"). This rule ensures that training, held-out and control donors share the same chemistry, the same processing pipeline and the same annotation, so that no integration across studies is needed and no difference between platforms enters the comparison. Platform effects, defined by Cable et al. (2022) as the effects of technology-dependent library preparation on the capture rate of individual genes, can otherwise dominate the biological signal and cause supervised methods to fail; restricting each tissue to one dataset removes them from every within-tissue comparison.

Datasets were sought in three repositories, CZ CELLxGENE Discover, the Broad Single Cell Portal and the Gene Expression Omnibus, and had to satisfy the following conditions (DECISIONS 2026-10-26, "Dataset"; refinement of 2026-10-27 *[pending Luca's approval]*). Each dataset had to include at least ten donors, so that a training split of several donors would remain after one held-out and one control donor had been set aside. Each had to provide, for every cell, a cell-type label and a donor label already assigned by the original authors, so that no re-annotation by the author of this thesis could influence the profiles or the truth; benchmarks built on single-cell references commonly adopt the same requirement, the Spotless benchmark stating that its datasets "already contain cell type annotations" (Sang-aram et al. 2024, Methods). For colon, only healthy tissue or colorectal cancer was admitted, and samples from inflammatory bowel disease were excluded. Finally, datasets whose processed data became public after the training cutoff of GEM-1 were preferred, because only such data allow the generative arm to be evaluated without the risk that the model has already encountered them (Section 1.6; criterion in Section 2.2.3).

### 2.2.2 The three datasets

For brain, the dataset is PsychAD, restricted to healthy adult donors and profiled by single-nucleus sequencing with 10x Genomics 3′ v3 chemistry in the dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (CZ CELLxGENE collection 84ce6837; Fullard et al. 2025). The selection yields approximately 200 donors *[exact number from `02_split.R`]* (CELLxGENE cell metadata, 2026-10-27). Nuclei rather than whole cells are an unavoidable consequence of the tissue, since healthy adult human brain is rarely profiled as dissociated cells, and nuclei contain only part of the cellular mRNA while still discriminating closely related cell types (Bakken et al. 2018). Because training, held-out and control donors are all nuclei of the same dataset, the difference between nuclei and cells does not enter any comparison made within the tissue.

For colon, the dataset is the colorectal cancer atlas of Pelka et al. (2021), restricted to the subset profiled with 10x Genomics 3′ v2 chemistry and to tumours with proficient mismatch repair (MMRp), which leaves 20 donors and 89,242 cells (CELLxGENE cell metadata, field `MMRStatusTumor`, 2026-10-27) *[restriction to MMRp tumours pending Luca's approval]*. Normal samples and mismatch-repair-deficient (MMRd) tumours are excluded because the two tumour types differ not only in composition, the predominant change being in the T-cell compartment, but also in the expression of the same cell type, with 16 of 43 malignant-cell programmes and several T-cell, myeloid and fibroblast programmes differing between them (Pelka et al. 2021). Since μ is a mean over the cells of one type, pooling the two subtypes would average two biological states, and the fidelity of empirical profiles would partly measure the mismatch of subtype between training and held-out donors. The original authors themselves analysed the two groups separately "to better capture differences between the two immunologically disparate tumor types" (Pelka et al. 2021, Results). MMRp was preferred to MMRd because it satisfies the rule of at least ten donors and retains more cell types with at least 50 cells across donors (CELLxGENE cell metadata, 2026-10-27). The colon results therefore describe MMRp tumours rather than colorectal cancer in general.

For lymphoid tissue, the dataset is the human lymph node atlas of Wells et al. (2025), profiled with 10x Genomics 5′ v2 chemistry and restricted to thoracic lymph nodes, which leaves 21 donors and 185,437 cells (CELLxGENE cell metadata, 2026-10-27) *[restriction to thoracic nodes pending Luca's approval]*. A single anatomical site provides a homogeneous biological context, since lymph nodes at different sites drain different organs, and the thoracic site was chosen because a larger body of literature is available for the biological validation of its profiles; the inguinal site, the only other candidate, had too few donors to be used on its own (CELLxGENE cell metadata, 2026-10-27). The 5′ chemistry is acceptable for the same reason as single nuclei for brain, as all comparisons are made within the tissue. Two consequences are nonetheless declared. The dataset contains immune cells only, so lymphoid pseudo-spots lack stromal and endothelial cells and describe mixtures of immune populations. In addition, GEM-1 may produce profiles closer to 3′ data if the technology cannot be specified in its query, which could disadvantage the generative arm in this tissue for technical rather than biological reasons (DECISIONS 2026-10-26, "Dataset").

None of the three references matches the chemistry of current spatial platforms exactly: Visium captures either the 3′ end of polyadenylated transcripts or, in its CytAssist version, hybridised probes *[REF: 10x Genomics product documentation]*. This does not affect the comparisons of this study, in which reference and pseudo-spots always share the same chemistry, but it limits the extent to which absolute errors can be transferred to real spatial data, a point taken up in the Discussion.

### 2.2.3 Eligibility for the generative arm

GEM-1-sc was trained on the version of CZ CELLxGENE Discover of July 2024, together with ten perturbation datasets from scPerturb (Koytiger et al. 2025, Methods). The proposed criterion is that a dataset counts as post-cutoff if its processed count matrix, with the cell labels, became public after July 2024 (DECISIONS 2026-10-27, cutoff *[pending Luca's approval; cutoff of the model served by the API to be confirmed at gate G0]*). Raw sequencing reads do not count, because the model learns from expression matrices with metadata rather than from reads that would first have to be processed and demultiplexed. Both dates are nevertheless recorded for each dataset. The pooled raw reads of PsychAD were released on 22 April 2024 (AD Knowledge Portal, Data Release 24.4) and its processed matrices in June 2025 (Data Release 25.6), so the dataset is post-cutoff under this criterion; the residual risk arising from the earlier release of the raw reads is declared in the Discussion. The lymph node data of Wells et al. (2025) became public on 6 June 2025 (GEO GSE299043) and are post-cutoff. The data of Pelka et al. (2021) became public on 26 August 2021 (GEO GSE178341) and are pre-cutoff, so the generative arm is not evaluated in colon (DECISIONS 2026-10-26, arm G; DECISIONS 2026-10-27, cutoff). The arm itself enters the study only if gate G0 is passed by 30 October 2026, which requires written permission to publish, working access to the API within the available quota, coverage of the tissues and cell types of the three datasets by the model's vocabulary, and a known cutoff date (PROTOCOLLO §9) *[outcome of G0 to be added]*.

## 2.3 Partition of donors and filtering of the real data

### 2.3.1 Partition by donor

The data of every tissue are partitioned by donor and never by cell (DECISIONS 2026-10-14; PROTOCOLLO §3.6). A partition by cell, as adopted in the Spotless benchmark, places cells of the same individuals in both the reference and the synthetic spots (Sang-aram et al. 2024), so that the variability between individuals, which a real application always faces, is absent from the evaluation and the results are optimistic. Partitioning by donor is stricter, and it is the donor-to-donor variability that this partition preserves which the control arm E′ is designed to measure. In colon, all samples of a donor are assigned to the same split, so that, for instance, the two tumour lesions of donor C130 cannot appear on both sides of the partition (DECISIONS 2026-10-26, colon split).

The proposed rule assigns, in each tissue, one donor to the held-out split and one to the E′ split, in accordance with the protocol, which refers to "the held-out donor" and to "a second independent real donor" (PROTOCOLLO §5.2), and assigns all other eligible donors to training (DECISIONS 2026-10-29, split *[pending Luca's approval]*). Before the assignment, donors whose number of cells is a low outlier, below the median minus three median absolute deviations on the logarithmic scale, are excluded from the whole dataset; this is the convention used for the quality control of cells (Amezquita et al. 2020), applied here to donors, and it removes seven brain donors with 61 to 770 nuclei and none in colon or lymphoid tissue. The held-out and E′ donors are then drawn at random, with a seed fixed before any result, among the remaining donors, so that the held-out donor is a typical donor of its dataset rather than the best sampled one. Training thus comprises 199 donors in brain, 18 in colon and 19 in lymphoid tissue (`scripts/02_split.R`). The resulting differences in training size do not affect the analyses, which are made separately within each tissue, but they are confounded with tissue and chemistry in any comparison between tissues, a limitation declared in the Discussion. Should a step of the pipeline prove unable to handle the full training split of brain, cells, never donors, will be subsampled within that split with a fixed seed, and the change will be recorded as a dated amendment before any result is produced.

The partition is produced by a single script, `02_split.R`, which also runs an automatic test that no donor identifier appears in more than one split (PROTOCOLLO §5.6). The training split supplies the empirical and model-based profiles, the dispersions of the noise model, the calibration of the parametric arm and the reference given to every method; the held-out donor supplies the target of fidelity, the noise ceiling and the anchor; the E′ donor supplies the profiles of the corresponding arm.

### 2.3.2 Filtering before the profiles are estimated

The protocol does not prescribe a filter on the real data, and the following minimal filter is proposed *[pending Luca's approval]*. It follows the rules applied by Crowell et al. (2023, Additional file 1, §6.1.2) to their real references, filtered because "insufficient numbers of cells and low-quality observations can interfere with estimation of simulation parameters", with cell types taking the place of their batches and clusters. A cell type is removed from a split if it has fewer than 50 cells in that split, and the removed types are reported; genes are retained if they have a count greater than one in at least ten cells; and cells are retained if they have at least 100 detected genes. The list of genes is determined on the training split only and applied unchanged to every split and arm, so that all matrices μ share the same genes and the held-out donor does not influence the choice. The number of cell types retained in each tissue is therefore known only after filtering *[from the real data]*. The downloaded files are never modified; filtered data are written as new files in a separate directory (PROTOCOLLO §3.7).

## 2.4 Data-generating mechanisms

### 2.4.1 The six expression sources

The empirical arm E uses as μ the mean expression of each gene in each cell type computed on the training split, and it represents the practice of taking profiles directly from a real reference. The parametric arm P draws gene means de novo from a gamma distribution and introduces marker genes with controlled fold-changes, following the logic of the Splatter framework (Zappia et al. 2017); it offers maximal control and minimal realism. The values of its parameters, the number of markers and their fold-change *[from the real data; whether the gamma parameters are set a priori or calibrated on the mean–variance range of arm E is pending Luca's approval]*. The model-based arm M uses the means of scDesign3 fitted to the training split (Song et al. 2024). The generative arm G uses the mean profiles returned by GEM-1-sc in its mean-estimation mode, queried with public metadata only, namely Cell Ontology and UBERON terms for cell type and tissue, as required by the terms of use of the provider (PROTOCOLLO §9); it is evaluated only in brain and lymphoid tissue (Section 2.2.3) and only if gate G0 is passed.

Two arms serve as internal controls. The negative control C− uses the profiles of arm E with gene labels permuted within each cell type, which preserves the distribution of expression values of each type while destroying its biological content; it is expected to fail, and if the ranking of methods did not change even with such profiles, the benchmark would be insensitive to expression and this would be reported as a result. A new permutation is drawn in every replicate, so that the conclusions do not depend on one particular shuffle *[pending Luca's approval]*. The arm E′ uses the mean profiles of the second independent donor. It is not a ceiling but a reference for donor-to-donor variability: a source close to E′ is as good as another real donor (DECISIONS 2026-10-26, E′). A synthetic arm may legitimately reach or exceed E′, because M is fitted on many training donors and G on public archives, so that both estimate a population-average profile, which can be closer to a new individual than a single other individual is. Exceeding E′ is therefore not evidence of leakage; the ceiling of fidelity is defined separately in Section 2.6.1, and leakage is checked on the provenance of the data (Section 2.6.5).

### 2.4.2 Generation of a pseudo-spot dataset

Within each replicate, a dataset of pseudo-spots *[number of spots to be fixed from the pilot]* is generated in three steps, identical for every arm (PROTOCOLLO §5.2; DECISIONS 2026-10-14). First, for each spot a vector of cell-type proportions is drawn from a Dirichlet distribution with concentration α *[from the real data, pending Luca's approval]*, and the cells of the spot are assigned to types accordingly. Second, for each cell of type k, the count of gene g is drawn from a negative binomial distribution with mean μ_gk, scaled by the library size of the cell *[library-size distribution from the real data]*, and dispersion φ_g estimated on the training split. Third, the counts of the cells of each spot are summed. Only μ differs between arms; the negative binomial noise model, which describes droplet data with UMIs adequately without zero inflation (Svensson 2020), is the same for all of them, so that differences in performance cannot arise from differences in the noise.

The two secondary factors that define the difficulty of the task each take two levels: the number of cells per spot and the sequencing depth *[levels from the real data]*. The levels are derived from the real datasets rather than chosen arbitrarily (PROTOCOLLO §5.2), and the lower depth level is checked against the minimum count of 100 UMIs below which RCTD discards a spot (Section 2.5), so that differences between methods are not driven by a built-in filter rather than by deconvolution. With five arms crossed with three tissues and four combinations of the secondary factors, plus arm G in two tissues, the design comprises 68 data-generating mechanisms, or 60 without the generative arm (ADEMP §2.2).

### 2.4.3 The real anchor

The anchor provides the benchmark against which the rankings of the simulated arms are judged. Its pseudo-spots are built by summing real cells of the held-out donor according to the same Dirichlet compositions used for the simulated arms, so that the true composition is known, because the type of every cell is known, while the expression is entirely real. Each anchor spot is built within a single sample, since a real spot never contains cells from two samples (DECISIONS 2026-10-26, colon split). Because real cells carry their own counts, the depth factor cannot be imposed by the generator; the proposal is to downsample the anchor spots at random to the same target depth as the simulated arms *[pending Luca's approval]*. Like the other arms, the anchor is generated in every combination of tissue, cells per spot and depth, giving twelve anchor conditions.

### 2.4.4 Common random numbers

Within replicate i, all arms and the anchor share the same spot compositions and the same assignment of cell types to the cells of each spot; only μ, and therefore the counts, differs. This follows from the protocol, which prescribes that only the source of μ varies and that the anchor uses the same Dirichlet compositions (PROTOCOLLO §5.2). The use of common random numbers induces a positive correlation between the results of different arms within a replicate, which reduces the Monte Carlo variance of between-arm comparisons (Morris et al. 2019, §5.3). In the same spirit, every method is applied to the same simulated datasets.

## 2.5 Deconvolution methods

Five methods are compared (DECISIONS 2026-10-26, methods). They were chosen for what they do and for their performance in independent benchmarks rather than for their popularity, which, as Crowell et al. (2023) note in the case of simulators, need not speak for or against performance. RCTD and cell2location were the two best-performing methods overall in the Spotless benchmark (Sang-aram et al. 2024), and SpatialDWLS, cell2location and RCTD were the best in the benchmark of Li et al. (2022). Together they span three statistical formulations: a Poisson likelihood with correction for platform effects (RCTD), a two-step weighted least-squares approach (SpatialDWLS) and a Bayesian negative binomial model of absolute abundances (cell2location). Two baselines complete the set. NNLS regresses each spot on the reference profiles under a non-negativity constraint, and in Spotless a basic regression of this kind outperformed almost half of the dedicated spatial methods (Sang-aram et al. 2024). MuSiC, developed for bulk tissue, weights genes by their consistency across the subjects of a multi-subject reference (Wang et al. 2019), and it was strong in accuracy but among the least stable when the reference changed in Spotless (Sang-aram et al. 2024).

CARD is not included. Its advantage derives from the spatial correlation of composition between neighbouring spots (Ma & Zhou 2022), which pseudo-spots without positions by construction do not possess; with artificial coordinates it would be penalised for reasons unrelated to its merit. Its exclusion is not a judgement of performance, since it was among the best methods in the evaluation of Li et al. (2023). SPOTlight, optional in the original protocol, was also removed (DECISIONS 2026-10-26, methods).

Every method receives the same reference, the training split of the tissue, in every arm. Methods are run with their default parameters, following the authors' recommendations where available, as in Crowell et al. (2023), and any departure is pre-registered. RCTD is run with the R package spacexr in full mode, which Cable et al. (2022) recommend when spots may contain three or more cell types, as pseudo-spots of several cells do; because the package default is doublet mode, the setting is stated explicitly *[pending Luca's approval]*. Positions are not required, and placeholder coordinates are supplied to the constructor of the spatial object. cell2location runs in Python on the GPU of the server; it receives raw counts, the expected number of cells per location is set to the true level of each condition, and its detection hyper-parameter is fixed in advance *[value pending Luca's approval]*. SpatialDWLS is run through the standalone R package spatialDWLS (version 0.1.1), which implements the two rounds of Dong and Yuan (2021) without the Giotto framework: candidate cell types are first identified for each cluster of spots by enrichment, and each spot is then solved by dampened weighted least squares restricted to those types. The signature matrix and marker genes are derived from the training split, the clustering of spots is fixed in advance *[algorithm and resolution to be fixed from the pilot]*, and the number of cells per spot is set to the true level of each condition. This re-implementation differs from the Giotto version evaluated in earlier benchmarks, and its version is recorded. MuSiC receives the donor label of every training cell, which its weighting requires. NNLS is implemented directly in R. Whether the two baselines enter the rankings or are reported alongside the three dedicated methods remains to be decided *[pending Luca's approval; proposal: rank all five, so that the baselines show whether the dedicated methods outperform a simple approach]*.

The built-in filters of each method are left at their defaults, because they are part of the pipeline a user would run. RCTD discards spots with fewer than 100 UMIs and requires at least 25 reference cells per cell type and integer counts (spacexr source code); cell2location applies no filter to spots but filters genes in the reference, as in its official tutorial; MuSiC drops genes with zero mean in the spots; NNLS applies none; the filter of spatialDWLS is to be verified in its source *[to be verified]*. These defaults, checked in the development versions of the packages, will be re-checked against the versions recorded in the software environment (Section 2.9). Spots discarded by a method are treated as failures (Section 2.8).

## 2.6 Estimands and performance measures

### 2.6.1 Fidelity of the expression sources

The estimand of the first aim is the cell-type profile μ and its marker genes, compared with the profile of the same cell type in the held-out donor. Fidelity is measured separately for each arm, tissue and cell type, rather than on all cell types together, because cell types differ in size and complexity and a global summary would be dominated by the most abundant ones; Crowell et al. (2023) averaged group-level results for the same reason. Three measures are used (PROTOCOLLO §5.5). The first is the correlation between the log-means of the source and those of the held-out donor, computed on the gene list of Section 2.3.2 *[correlation coefficient and pseudocount pending Luca's approval]*. The second is the overlap of the top-k marker genes of each cell type *[k pending Luca's approval]*. The third is the separability of the cell types *[metric pending Luca's approval]*.

A correlation with the held-out donor cannot reach one even for a perfect source, because the held-out profile is itself a mean of a finite number of noisy cells. The attainable maximum is estimated by a noise ceiling (DECISIONS 2026-10-26, noise ceiling), an idea borrowed from representational similarity analysis (Nili et al. 2014) *[REF: verify]*. For each tissue and cell type, the cells of the held-out donor are divided at random into two halves of equal size within the cell type, the extra cell of an odd count being assigned to one half at random, and the correlation r between the two half-profiles is computed with the same coefficient and genes as above. The Spearman–Brown correction 2r/(1 + r) *[REF]* extrapolates this split-half reliability to the full set of cells, under the assumption of equal halves that the stratified split guarantees, and its square root is the highest correlation that any source can reach with the held-out profile. The division is repeated B times and the results are averaged *[B and seed to be fixed from the pilot]*. Cell types with few held-out cells have a lower ceiling, and fidelity is read against it.

### 2.6.2 Error of deconvolution

The primary estimand of the second aim is the proportion π_sk of cell type k in spot s. Unlike the scalar parameter θ of the framework of Morris et al. (2019), it is a vector for each spot, and its estimation error is summarised at two levels. Within replicate i, the root mean squared error RMSE_i is the square root of the mean of (π̂_sk − π_sk)² over all spots and cell types, and the Jensen–Shannon divergence JSD_i is the mean over spots of the divergence between the estimated and the true composition. Across replicates, each measure is averaged over the n_sim replicates and reported with its Monte Carlo standard error, SD(RMSE_i)/√n_sim, and likewise for JSD. Errors are computed for every combination of method, arm and condition, and on the anchor. RMSE is proposed as the primary measure and JSD as the secondary one *[pending Luca's approval]*.

### 2.6.3 Rankings and their agreement

Within each arm and condition, the methods are ranked by their mean RMSE and, secondarily, by their mean JSD. The agreement between the ranking of each arm and that of the anchor is measured with Kendall's τ, and the agreement among all arms with Kendall's W (PROTOCOLLO §5.5). Their Monte Carlo standard errors are obtained by bootstrap over replicates *[number of bootstrap samples to be fixed from the pilot]*. With five methods there are only ten pairs, so that τ takes eleven values and a single exchange between two methods lowers it from 1 to 0.8; with three ranked methods a single exchange lowers it to 0.33. For this reason the pairwise differences in RMSE between methods are also reported with their Monte Carlo standard errors, so that an exchange within Monte Carlo error is not interpreted as a real change of ranking.

### 2.6.4 Decision rules

The rules that turn the hypotheses of Section 2.1 into decisions, namely when a ranking differs from the anchor and when an arm is the closest to it, are fixed in the protocol before the results *[pending Luca's approval; proposal: a ranking differs when a pairwise difference between methods changes sign beyond twice its Monte Carlo standard error, and the closest arm is the one with the highest τ, ties broken by the mean absolute difference in RMSE from the anchor]*.

### 2.6.5 Counter-proofs

Three checks are planned to falsify the results if they were wrong (PROTOCOLLO §5.6, as amended by DECISIONS 2026-10-26). The negative control C− must yield low fidelity and rankings that differ from the anchor. A synthetic source above E′ is not taken as evidence of leakage and is reported with its explanation; for M and G this is the averaging over many donors described in Section 2.4.1, whereas P, built de novo, has no such explanation, and a result of P above E′ is examined further. A source whose fidelity exceeds the noise ceiling beyond Monte Carlo error indicates an error or leakage and is investigated before the analysis proceeds. Leakage is checked on the provenance of the data in every case, not only when a source appears too good (DECISIONS 2026-10-26, leakage): an automatic test verifies that no donor appears in more than one split, and arm G is evaluated only on donors whose processed data became public after its training cutoff.

## 2.7 Number of replicates and Monte Carlo error

The number of replicates is chosen to reach a stated precision of the key performance measure rather than fixed by convention. Earlier benchmarks of deconvolution fixed the number of replicates without a precision target, for instance ten replicates in Spotless (Sang-aram et al. 2024) and five per setting in the simulations of CARD (Ma & Zhou 2022), whereas the comparison of simulators by Crowell et al. (2023) generated one simulated dataset for each combination of reference and simulator (repository HelenaLC/simulation-comparison, version available at the time of writing). Following Morris et al. (2019), the number of replicates is n_sim = (SD(RMSE_i)/target MCSE)², where SD(RMSE_i) is the standard deviation of the per-replicate error estimated in the pilot and the target Monte Carlo standard error is fixed in advance *[key measure, target MCSE and SD pending Luca's approval and the pilot]*. Because halving the Monte Carlo error requires four times as many replicates, the largest standard deviation across the cells of the design is used, so that every cell reaches the target. The computational cost, the product of the number of replicates, data-generating mechanisms, methods and the time of a single run, is checked against the resources available *[to be fixed from the pilot]*. cell2location, which runs on a shared GPU, may receive its own number of replicates, fixed by a dated amendment in the first days of work on the server and before any result (DECISIONS 2026-10-14). The fidelity of arms E and E′ has no Monte Carlo error, since their μ is fixed once the partition is made; Monte Carlo standard errors are reported for fidelity only where μ is random, in arms P, M, C− and possibly G *[pending Luca's approval; departs from PROTOCOLLO §5.5, which asks for a Monte Carlo standard error for every measure]*.

The pilot that supplies these quantities is declared in advance and its numbers do not enter the results. It is run on one tissue, with arms E and C−, the four methods that run on a CPU (RCTD, SpatialDWLS, MuSiC and NNLS) and a small number of replicates *[tissue and number pending Luca's approval]*, and it provides the standard deviation of the error, the running time per run, the types of failure that occur and the check on random seeds described in Section 2.9.

## 2.8 Treatment of failed runs

No quality filter is applied to the pseudo-spots, since the real cells are filtered upstream (Section 2.3.2) and adaptive filters designed to remove damaged cells and empty droplets have no target in spots built from filtered cells; spots with few counts are the designed low-depth condition, and removing them would remove the hardest cases. Each method applies only its own built-in filter, as a user running the published pipeline would.

Following Morris et al. (2019, §5.1), failures are never discarded silently, because they tend to occur in the hardest datasets and their removal would make a method appear better than it is. A failure is a spot that a method discards or cannot process, a run that does not converge, or an estimated composition whose proportions do not sum to one within a tolerance *[tolerance pending Luca's approval]*. Failures are counted for every combination of method, arm and condition and reported as a result. In the main analysis, methods are compared on the spots returned by all methods in the same replicate, so that every method is scored on the same spots. In a sensitivity analysis, failed spots receive the worst possible score, RMSE, τ and W are recomputed, and any change in the ranking is reported. Failures may still differ between arms through μ, as is expected for C−; the types of failure that actually occur are identified in the pilot *[to be fixed from the pilot]*.

## 2.9 Software and reproducibility

All analyses are written in R *[version from `renv.lock`]*, with the exception of cell2location, which runs in a dedicated Python environment on the server *[environment and versions pending Luca's approval]*. The versions of all R packages are recorded in a lock file managed by renv, from which the environment is rebuilt on the server. Paths to the data are read from an environment variable and never written in the code, so that the same scripts run unchanged on a personal computer and on the server (PROTOCOLLO §4.2).

The real data are obtained only through a script, `01_download.R`, which downloads each file of the CZ CELLxGENE Discover datasets in h5ad format, computes its MD5 checksum and writes, or compares if it already exists, a manifest recording the file, its URL, its size in bytes, its checksum and the date of download (PROTOCOLLO §4.2, step 7). The data themselves are never placed under version control, only the manifest. When the work moves to the server, the script is run again and the checksums are compared with the manifest, which constitutes the first check that the pipeline is reproducible; data are never copied from the personal computer to the server. Downloaded files are read-only, and every derived file is written to a separate directory. Every call to GEM-1 is logged with its date, the model identifier, the complete query and the MD5 checksum of the response, because the model may change over time and the log is the only means of reproducing arm G (PROTOCOLLO §9).

A single seed is fixed for the whole study *[seed set at approval]*, and the state of the random number generator is stored at the start of each replicate, as recommended by Morris et al. (2019). Parallel processes use independent streams derived from that seed with the L'Ecuyer-CMRG generator. Because some packages may reset the seed internally, which would make replicates correlated, the state of the generator is compared before and after two runs of each method in the pilot, and any such behaviour is corrected before the main study. The simulated datasets, or their seeds and generator states, are saved so that any replicate can be regenerated. Every figure and table is produced by a single command from the scripts and the data, and no file is edited by hand between one script and the next (PROTOCOLLO §3.5). Each stage of the work is closed by a report with a fixed structure, comprising the pre-registration, checks of software correctness, checks of biological plausibility and a counter-proof, and its key numbers are recomputed by a reviewer other than the author before the stage is closed (PROTOCOLLO §3.4, §7). Any change to the protocol after its approval is recorded as a dated amendment, written before the results it could affect, and the deviations are listed in an appendix.
