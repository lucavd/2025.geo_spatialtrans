This chapter describes the simulation study designed to answer the question raised in Chapter 1. The study follows the ADEMP framework of Morris et al. (2019). Its protocol, including hypotheses, decision rules and the number of replicates, was written and approved before any result was examined *[date of approval to be added]*. After an overview of the design, the chapter follows the order of the work: the real datasets and the reasons for choosing them, the partition of their donors and the filtering of their cells, the mechanisms that generate the simulated data, the deconvolution methods, the estimands and performance measures, the number of replicates, the treatment of failed runs, and the software environment that makes every result reproducible.

## 2.1 Overview of the design

Morris et al. (2019) recommend that a simulation study specify five components in advance: aims, data-generating mechanisms, estimands, methods and performance measures. The two aims of this study are those stated in Section 1.7. The first aim concerns fidelity. Each expression source produces a matrix μ of mean expression, with one row per gene and one column per cell type, and the question is how closely this matrix approximates the profiles of the same tissue and cell type measured in real donors who did not contribute to its construction. The second aim concerns the sensitivity of the benchmark. It asks whether the error and the ranking of deconvolution methods change with the expression source, and which source yields the ranking closest to the one obtained on pseudo-spots built from real cells of a held-out donor, called the real anchor.

The design varies one principal factor, the source of μ, across six arms. Every other part of the data-generating process is shared: the generator, the noise model, the spot compositions and the reference given to the methods are identical in all arms. This answers the limitation discussed in Section 1.5. In the comparison of Crowell et al. (2023) each simulator combined its own expression model with its own noise model, so a change in the ranking of methods could not be traced to one component. Here, any difference between arms can be traced to the expression source alone. Three secondary factors, namely tissue, number of cells per spot and sequencing depth, are crossed with the principal factor in a fixed grid, so that the conclusions can be examined in settings of different composition and difficulty.

Three hypotheses were stated before the study. The first concerns the negative control: the arm with permuted gene labels should show the lowest fidelity in every tissue and a ranking of methods different from that of the anchor. The second concerns the arm built from a second real donor. This arm shows how close one real individual comes to another, in fidelity and in agreement with the anchor ranking, and so provides the scale on which the other arms are read. It is not a ceiling. The third is the principal hypothesis of the thesis: at least one synthetic arm yields a ranking of methods that differs from the ranking obtained on the anchor. Section 2.6.4 turns "differs" and "closest" into explicit decision rules.

## 2.2 Real datasets and their selection

### 2.2.1 Selection criteria

The real data have three roles. They provide the training data from which the empirical and model-based profiles, the dispersions of the noise model and the reference for the methods are estimated. They provide the held-out donors against which fidelity is measured and from whose cells the anchor is built. They provide the independent donor of the arm E′. Within each tissue, all three roles are filled by different donors of one dataset (DECISIONS 2026-10-26, "Dataset"). Training, held-out and comparator donors therefore share chemistry, processing pipeline and annotation, no integration across studies is needed, and no difference between platforms enters the comparison. This matters because platform effects, defined by Cable et al. (2022) as the effects of technology-dependent library preparation on the capture rate of individual genes, can dominate the biological signal and make supervised methods fail.

Datasets were sought in CZ CELLxGENE Discover, the Broad Single Cell Portal and the Gene Expression Omnibus. Each had to include at least ten donors, so that a training split of several donors would remain after the held-out donors had been set aside. Each had to provide, for every cell, a cell-type label and a donor label assigned by the original authors, so that no re-annotation could influence the profiles or the truth. Spotless adopts the same requirement for its single-cell references, whose datasets "already contain cell type annotations" (Sang-aram et al. 2024, Methods). The cell type used throughout is the authors' label, not the harmonised Cell Ontology term of CELLxGENE: `ClusterMidway` for colon, `author_cell_type` for lymphoid tissue and `subclass` for brain. For colon, only healthy tissue or colorectal cancer was admitted, not inflammatory bowel disease. The rules and the three datasets chosen were approved by the supervisor on 30 October 2026 (DECISIONS 2026-10-26, "Dataset", refined 2026-10-27).

### 2.2.2 The three datasets

For brain, the dataset is PsychAD, single-nucleus sequencing of the dorsolateral prefrontal cortex with 10x Genomics 3′ v3 chemistry (Fullard et al. 2025). It is restricted to healthy adults, that is donors with disease status "normal" and age of at least 18 years, excluding donors of unknown age, which leaves 208 donors (`scripts/02_split.R`). Healthy adult human brain is rarely profiled as dissociated cells. Nuclei contain only part of the cellular mRNA but still discriminate closely related cell types (Bakken et al. 2018), and since all donors of the tissue are nuclei of the same dataset, the difference between nuclei and cells does not enter any within-tissue comparison.

For colon, the dataset is the colorectal cancer atlas of Pelka et al. (2021), restricted to the subset profiled with 10x Genomics 3′ v2 chemistry and to tumours with proficient mismatch repair (MMRp). This leaves 20 donors and 89,242 cells (CELLxGENE cell metadata, field `MMRStatusTumor`, 2026-10-27). Normal samples and mismatch-repair-deficient (MMRd) tumours are excluded because the two tumour types differ in composition, mainly in the T-cell compartment, and also in the expression of the same cell type: 16 of 43 malignant-cell programmes differ between them, as do several T-cell, myeloid and fibroblast programmes (Pelka et al. 2021). Since μ is a mean over the cells of one type, pooling the two subtypes would average two biological states, and the fidelity of the empirical profiles would partly measure a mismatch of subtype between donors. The original authors analysed the two groups separately "to better capture differences between the two immunologically disparate tumor types" (Pelka et al. 2021, Results). MMRp was preferred to MMRd because it meets the rule of ten donors and retains more cell types with at least 50 cells across donors (CELLxGENE cell metadata, 2026-10-27). The colon results therefore describe MMRp tumours, not colorectal cancer in general.

For lymphoid tissue, the dataset is the human lymph node atlas of Wells et al. (2025), profiled with 10x Genomics 5′ v2 chemistry and restricted to thoracic lymph nodes. This leaves 21 donors and 185,437 cells (CELLxGENE cell metadata, 2026-10-27). One anatomical site gives a homogeneous biological context, because lymph nodes at different sites drain different organs, and the thoracic site has a larger literature for the biological checks of the profiles. The 5′ chemistry is acceptable for the same reason as nuclei in brain, since every comparison is made within the tissue. Two consequences are declared. The dataset contains immune cells only, so lymphoid pseudo-spots lack stromal and endothelial cells and describe mixtures of immune populations. In addition, if the query to GEM-1 cannot specify the 5′ chemistry, its profiles may resemble 3′ data, which could disadvantage the generative arm in this tissue for technical rather than biological reasons (DECISIONS 2026-10-26).

None of the three references matches the chemistry of current spatial platforms exactly, since Visium captures either the 3′ end of polyadenylated transcripts or, in its CytAssist version, hybridised probes *[REF: 10x Genomics product documentation]*. This does not affect the comparisons of this study, in which reference and pseudo-spots always share their chemistry, but it limits how far absolute errors transfer to real spatial data, a point taken up in the Discussion.

### 2.2.3 Eligibility for the generative arm

GEM-1-sc was trained on the whole of CZ CELLxGENE Discover as of July 2024 (Koytiger et al. 2025, Methods). A dataset is therefore eligible for the generative arm if it is absent from the CELLxGENE Census of 1 July 2024. The supervisor checked that Census version, and none of the three datasets is present, so the generative arm can be evaluated in all three tissues (DECISIONS 2026-10-30, cutoff). One uncertainty remains and is declared: it is not known on which version of CELLxGENE the model currently served by the API was trained.

The generative arm has no deadline for entry. It enters the study when three conditions hold: written permission to publish from Synthesize Bio, working access to the API within the available quota, and coverage of the tissues and cell types of the three datasets by the model's vocabulary (DECISIONS 2026-10-30, entry of G). The decision is recorded with its date before any result of the arm is examined, so that the arm cannot be kept only when it performs well. If the conditions are not met in time, the arm is removed and its absence is declared as a limitation *[status of arm G to be added]*.

## 2.3 Partition of donors and filtering of the real data

### 2.3.1 Partition by donor

The data of every tissue are partitioned by donor, never by cell (DECISIONS 2026-10-14; PROTOCOLLO §3.6). A partition by cell, as in Spotless (Sang-aram et al. 2024), puts cells of the same individuals in both the reference and the synthetic spots. The variability between individuals, which every real application faces, is then absent and the results are optimistic. The partition by donor keeps this variability, and the arm E′ measures it. In colon, all samples of a donor go to the same split; for instance, the two tumour lesions of donor C130 cannot appear on both sides (DECISIONS 2026-10-26, colon split).

Before the partition, donors whose number of cells is a low outlier are removed from the whole dataset. The threshold is the median minus three median absolute deviations on the logarithmic scale, the convention used for the quality control of cells (Amezquita et al. 2020) applied here to donors. It removes seven brain donors, with 61 to 770 nuclei and 3,335 nuclei in all, and no donor in colon or lymphoid tissue (DECISIONS 2026-10-29, split). Among the remaining donors, four are drawn at random with a seed fixed before any result (20261029). They form the held-out group. The first donor drawn is the held-out donor of the second aim and the second is the E′ donor; all other donors form the training split (DECISIONS 2026-10-29, split; DECISIONS 2026-10-30, held-out group). A random draw makes the held-out donor a typical donor of its dataset rather than the best sampled one. Training thus contains 197 donors in brain, 16 in colon and 17 in lymphoid tissue (`scripts/02_split.R`). The held-out group is Donor_207, Donor_680, Donor_307 and Donor_957 in brain, C104, C105, C134 and C162 in colon, and 583B, 591C, D543 and D512 in lymphoid tissue.

The training split supplies the empirical and model-based profiles, the dispersions and library sizes of the noise model, the calibration of the parametric arm and the reference given to every method. The held-out donors supply the target of fidelity and the noise ceiling, and the first of them supplies the anchor. The E′ donor supplies the profiles of the arm E′. The sizes of the training splits differ between tissues because the same rule, all eligible donors except the held-out group, is applied in each. This does not affect the analyses, which are made within each tissue, but it is confounded with tissue and chemistry in any comparison between tissues. If a step of the pipeline cannot handle the full training split of brain, which holds about one million nuclei, cells, never donors, are subsampled within that split with a fixed seed, and the change is recorded as a dated amendment before any result (DECISIONS 2026-10-29). The script `02_split.R` produces the partition and runs an automatic test that no donor identifier appears in more than one split.

### 2.3.2 Cell types and filtering

A cell type enters a role only if it has enough cells there to estimate a reliable mean (DECISIONS 2026-10-29, cell types). The threshold is 50 cells, following Crowell et al. (2023, Additional file 1, §6.1.2), who filtered their real references because "insufficient numbers of cells and low-quality observations can interfere with estimation of simulation parameters"; their groups were batches and clusters, ours are cell types. The threshold defines three sets of types in each tissue. The expression sources and the reference given to the methods use every type with at least 50 training cells: 27 in brain, 18 in colon and 30 in lymphoid tissue. The first aim scores the types with at least 50 cells in the held-out donor (20, 8 and 9 for the first held-out donor). The other types are reported as not evaluated rather than penalised, because no reliable true profile exists for them. The pseudo-spots of every arm, and those of the anchor, use the types with at least 50 cells in training, in the held-out donor and in the E′ donor: 11 in brain, 6 in colon and 7 in lymphoid tissue. This last restriction is needed because E′ lacks a profile for some types of the held-out donor, and it ensures that every arm, E′ included, uses the same compositions.

The reference keeps all training types, so it contains more types than the spots. This mirrors the use of a real atlas, which usually contains more types than a given tissue section. Reference types absent from the spots have a true proportion of zero, and any proportion assigned to them counts as error. The opposite case, a type present in the spots but absent from the reference, is not allowed. Avila Cobos et al. (2020) showed that removing one cell type from the reference increased the deconvolution error at least two-fold in several settings, and in machine-learning terms a test class unseen in training is a form of dataset shift (Moreno-Torres et al. 2012).

Two further filters are proposed *[pending Luca's approval]*. Genes are kept if they have a count greater than one in at least ten cells, and cells are kept if they have at least 100 detected genes. The gene list is fixed on the training split and applied unchanged to every split and arm, so that all matrices μ share the same genes and the held-out donors do not influence the choice. The downloaded files are never modified, and filtered data are written as new files (PROTOCOLLO §3.7).

## 2.4 Data-generating mechanisms

### 2.4.1 The six expression sources

The empirical arm E takes μ directly from the training split and represents the practice of using a real reference as the source of profiles. The parametric arm P draws gene means de novo from a gamma distribution and adds marker genes with controlled fold-changes, following the logic of Splatter (Zappia et al. 2017); it offers maximal control and minimal realism *[gamma parameters, number of markers and fold-change from the real data; a priori or calibrated on arm E pending Luca's approval]*. The model-based arm M uses the means of scDesign3 fitted to the training split (Song et al. 2024). The generative arm G uses the mean profiles returned by GEM-1-sc in its mean-estimation mode, queried with public metadata only, namely Cell Ontology and UBERON terms for cell type and tissue, as the provider's terms of use require (PROTOCOLLO §9).

Two arms serve as internal controls. The negative control C− takes the profiles of arm E and permutes the gene labels within each cell type. This keeps the distribution of expression values of each type and destroys its biological content. A new permutation is drawn in every replicate, so the conclusions do not depend on one shuffle (DECISIONS 2026-10-30). C− is expected to fail; if the ranking of methods did not change even with such profiles, the benchmark would be insensitive to expression, and this would be reported as a result. The arm E′ takes the mean profiles of the independent comparator donor. No parameter is tuned on this donor, so it is not a validation set (DECISIONS 2026-10-29, E′). E′ is a reference for donor-to-donor variability: a source close to E′ is as good as another real donor (DECISIONS 2026-10-26, E′). A synthetic arm may legitimately reach or exceed E′. M is fitted on many training donors and G on public archives, so both estimate a population-average profile, which can be closer to a new individual than a single other individual is. Exceeding E′ is therefore not evidence of leakage. The ceiling of fidelity is defined separately (Section 2.6.1), and leakage is checked on the provenance of the data (Section 2.6.5).

### 2.4.2 Profiles, library sizes and dispersion

In every arm μ is a relative profile. For arm E and type k, μ_gk is the sum of the counts of gene g over all training cells of type k divided by the sum of all counts of those cells, so that each column describes the share of the transcriptome taken by each gene (DECISIONS 2026-10-29, noise model). This pooled mean is the maximum-likelihood rate of a Poisson model with log library size as offset, and it has the same "profile times library size" structure as RCTD (Cable et al. 2022) and muscat (Crowell et al. 2020). The same definition is applied to the cells of the E′ donor and of each held-out donor.

The library size L of a simulated cell of type k is drawn at random from the library sizes of the training cells of that type, and the mean count of gene g is μ_gk × L. This empirical choice follows muscat, where simulated cells take the library sizes of reference cells of their group, rather than a parametric distribution such as the log-normal of Splatter (Zappia et al. 2017).

The dispersion φ_g is one value per gene, estimated on the training split with glmGamPoi (Ahlmann-Eltze & Huber 2020) under the design `~ cell_type + donor`, with log L as offset (DECISIONS 2026-10-29, noise model, with the donor covariate added by the supervisor on 2026-10-30). The donor term matters. Without it, φ_g would absorb the variability between donors, the simulated arms would be noisier than the anchor, which comes from one donor, and the difference would have nothing to do with μ. In every tissue the fit uses a sample of 20,000 training cells, stratified by cell type in proportion to each type's share and drawn with a fixed seed. One rule for all tissues is needed because the brain training split does not fit in memory, and glmGamPoi itself provides subsampling. The adequacy of the sample is checked in colon, where all training cells fit: the sample is adequate if the correlation of log φ between the sample and all cells is at least 0.99 across genes and the median ratio of the two estimates lies between 0.95 and 1.05; otherwise the sample is raised to 50,000 cells in every tissue. The fit uses the per-gene maximum-likelihood estimate without shrinkage, with genes fitted in parallel blocks, which gives the same estimates as a single fit (checked on 2026-10-31). A per-gene negative binomial dispersion is also used by muscat and by the model of cell2location (Kleshchevnikov et al. 2022).

### 2.4.3 Generation of a pseudo-spot dataset

Within each replicate a dataset of pseudo-spots is generated in three steps, identical for every arm (PROTOCOLLO §5.2). The number of spots per dataset is 500 in the pilot, the default of the synthspot simulator used by Spotless, and the final number is fixed from the pilot *[to be fixed from the pilot]*. First, the composition π_s of spot s over the K spot types is drawn from a Dirichlet distribution with α_k = K · p_k, where p_k is the share of type k among the training cells of the spot types, and the cells of the spot are assigned to types by a multinomial draw; the truth is the resulting fraction of cells of each type. With equal shares this reduces to the symmetric α = 1 of the Open Problems spatial-decomposition simulator. Proportions from training, rather than α = 1, make the spots resemble the tissue and avoid giving rare types as many cells as common ones, which would force the anchor to reuse the few held-out cells of rare types many times. They come from training, not from the held-out donor, so the test donor does not shape its own test. As a consequence, rare types appear rarely and the methods are tested less on them. Second, the count of gene g in each cell of type k is drawn from a negative binomial distribution with mean μ_gk × L and dispersion φ_g. Third, the counts of the cells of each spot are summed.

Only μ differs between arms. The negative binomial model, which describes droplet data with unique molecular identifiers without the need for zero inflation (Svensson 2020), is the same for all of them, so differences in performance cannot come from the noise.

### 2.4.4 Secondary factors

The number of cells per spot takes the values 2 and 10, the bounds of synthspot (`n_cells_min = 2`, `n_cells_max = 10`). Two cells represent a Visium HD bin of 8 µm, where bins typically mix two cell types (Oliveira et al. 2025), and ten represent a standard Visium spot, which holds 10 to 20 cells (Elosua-Bayes et al. 2021). The number is fixed within each level, so the methods that accept it can receive the true value (Section 2.5).

Sequencing depth is defined per cell, so that it stays crossed with the number of cells: the target of a spot is the number of its cells times the depth per cell. The two levels are 175 and 4,900 unique molecular identifiers (UMI) per cell. The low level comes from Visium HD: the mean UMI count per 8 µm bin under tissue is 207 and 488 in the two public 10x Genomics human colorectal cancer samples (Space Ranger `metrics_summary.csv` of `Visium_HD_Human_Colon_Cancer_P1` and `Visium_HD_Human_Colon_Cancer`, downloaded 2026-10-31), about 350 on average, divided by two cells. The high level comes from standard Visium: the median is 49,282 UMI per 55 µm spot in the 10x Genomics sample `CytAssist_11mm_FFPE_Human_Colorectal_Cancer`, divided by ten cells. Dividing by the two cell levels is an assumption, and it is declared. The spot targets are therefore 350 and 9,800 UMI for two-cell spots and 1,750 and 49,000 UMI for ten-cell spots. A spot above its target is thinned at random to the target, in every arm and in the anchor; a spot below its target is kept as it is, following synthspot, so the high level often equals the depth of the single-cell data. The lowest target, 350 UMI, stays above the 100-UMI threshold below which RCTD discards a spot.

With five arms, three tissues and four combinations of the secondary factors, the design comprises 60 data-generating mechanisms, or 72 if the generative arm enters (DECISIONS 2026-10-30). Each mechanism is matched by an anchor condition, twelve in all.

### 2.4.5 The real anchor

The anchor is the benchmark against which the rankings of the simulated arms are judged. Its pseudo-spots are built by summing real cells of the first held-out donor according to the same compositions used for the simulated arms. The true composition is known, because the type of every cell is known, while the expression is entirely real. Each anchor spot is built within a single sample, since a real spot never contains cells from two samples (DECISIONS 2026-10-26, colon split). Because real cells carry their own counts, the anchor spots are thinned at random to the same target depth as the simulated arms (DECISIONS 2026-10-30). The held-out donors are typical donors with a few thousand cells, for example 2,433 in lymphoid donor 583B and 2,886 in colon donor C104 (`scripts/02_split.R`), so the anchor reuses the same cells across spots and replicates, as other pseudo-spot simulators do. The pilot reports the expected reuse per cell for the chosen number of spots *[to be fixed from the pilot]*.

### 2.4.6 Common random numbers

Within replicate i, all arms and the anchor share the same spot compositions and the same assignment of types to the cells of each spot; only μ, and therefore the counts, differs. This follows from the protocol, which prescribes that only μ varies and that the anchor uses the same compositions (PROTOCOLLO §5.2). Common random numbers induce a positive correlation between the results of different arms in a replicate and so reduce the Monte Carlo variance of between-arm comparisons (Morris et al. 2019, §5.3). In the same way, every method is applied to the same simulated datasets.

## 2.5 Deconvolution methods

Five methods are compared (DECISIONS 2026-10-26, methods). They were chosen for what they do and for their results in independent benchmarks, not for their popularity, which, as Crowell et al. (2023) note for simulators, need not speak for or against performance. RCTD and cell2location were the two best methods overall in Spotless (Sang-aram et al. 2024), and SpatialDWLS, cell2location and RCTD were the best in the benchmark of Li et al. (2022). The three methods span three statistical formulations: a Poisson likelihood with a correction for platform effects (RCTD), a Bayesian negative binomial model of absolute cell abundances (cell2location) and a two-step weighted least-squares approach (SpatialDWLS). Two baselines complete the set. NNLS regresses each spot on the reference profiles under a non-negativity constraint; in Spotless a regression of this kind outperformed almost half of the dedicated methods. MuSiC, developed for bulk tissue, weights genes by their consistency across the subjects of a multi-subject reference (Wang et al. 2019); in Spotless it was accurate but among the least stable when the reference changed (Sang-aram et al. 2024). All five methods are ranked, so the baselines show whether the dedicated methods outperform a simple approach (DECISIONS 2026-10-30).

CARD is not included. Its advantage comes from the spatial correlation of composition between neighbouring spots (Ma & Zhou 2022), which pseudo-spots without positions do not have; with artificial coordinates it would be penalised for reasons unrelated to its merit. The exclusion is not a judgement of performance, since CARD was among the best methods in Li et al. (2023). SPOTlight, optional in the original protocol, was also removed (DECISIONS 2026-10-26, methods).

Every method receives the same reference in every arm, built from the training split of the tissue with at most 1,000 cells per cell type, drawn at random with a fixed seed; types with fewer cells keep all of them. Methods run with their default parameters, following the authors' recommendations where available, as in Crowell et al. (2023), and any departure is pre-registered. RCTD runs through the R package spacexr (version 2.2.1) in full mode, which Cable et al. (2022) recommend when spots may contain three or more cell types; because the package default is doublet mode, the setting is stated explicitly (DECISIONS 2026-10-30). The two-cell spots would suit doublet mode, but full mode also handles them and keeps one setting across conditions. Positions are not required, and placeholder coordinates are supplied. cell2location runs in Python on the GPU of the server and receives raw counts. Its expected number of cells per location is set to the true level of each condition, and its `detection_alpha` is 200 in the main analysis and 20 in a sensitivity analysis *[pending Luca's approval]*. The value 200 is the default that Kleshchevnikov et al. (2022) recommend when detection sensitivity varies little across locations, and it is the value used by Spotless for synthetic spots built, as ours are, from the same technology as the reference; the value 20 is their recommendation for strong gradients of detection in real Visium data, which pseudo-spots do not have. SpatialDWLS runs through the standalone R package spatialDWLS (version 0.1.1), which implements the two rounds of Dong and Yuan (2021) without the Giotto framework: candidate cell types are first identified for each cluster of spots by enrichment, and each spot is then solved by dampened weighted least squares restricted to those types. Its signature and marker genes come from the training split, its number of cells per spot is the true level, and the spots are clustered by k-means on principal components *[settings to be fixed from the pilot]*. This re-implementation differs from the Giotto version evaluated in earlier benchmarks, and its version is recorded. MuSiC (version 1.0.0) receives the donor label of every training cell, which its weighting requires. NNLS uses the R package nnls (version 1.6).

Giving the true number of cells per spot to the methods that accept it, spatialDWLS and cell2location, is oracle information that real data do not provide, and it is declared as such (DECISIONS 2026-10-30). The built-in filters of each method are left at their defaults, because they are part of the pipeline a user runs. RCTD discards spots with fewer than 100 UMI and requires at least 25 reference cells per type and integer counts; cell2location filters genes in the reference but not spots; MuSiC drops genes with zero mean in the spots; NNLS has no filter; the filter of spatialDWLS is to be checked in its source *[to be verified]*. These defaults were read in the development versions of the packages and will be re-checked against the installed versions (Section 2.9). Spots discarded by a method are failures (Section 2.8).

## 2.6 Estimands and performance measures

### 2.6.1 Fidelity of the expression sources

The estimand of the first aim is the cell-type profile μ, compared with the profile of the same type in a held-out donor. Fidelity is measured for each arm, tissue and cell type, rather than on all types together, because types differ in size and complexity and a global summary would be dominated by the most abundant ones; Crowell et al. (2023) averaged group-level results for the same reason. It is computed against each of the four donors of the held-out group, and E′ is computed on all six pairs of the group. This gives a distribution of donor-to-donor fidelity instead of a single number (DECISIONS 2026-10-30, held-out group). Of the arms, only M must be refitted for each target donor; E, P, C− and G do not depend on it.

The main measure is the Pearson correlation between the log-profiles of the source and of the held-out donor, on the gene list of Section 2.3.2, with the Spearman correlation as a sensitivity analysis (DECISIONS 2026-10-30) *[pseudocount pending Luca's approval]*. Two further measures follow PROTOCOLLO §5.5: the overlap of the top-k marker genes of each type and the separability of the cell types *[k and the separability metric pending Luca's approval]*.

A correlation with a held-out donor cannot reach one even for a perfect source, because the held-out profile is itself a mean of a finite number of noisy cells. The attainable maximum is estimated by a noise ceiling (DECISIONS 2026-10-26, noise ceiling), an idea taken from representational similarity analysis (Nili et al. 2014) *[REF: verify]*. For each tissue and cell type, the cells of the held-out donor are split at random into two halves of equal size, the extra cell of an odd count going to one half at random, and the correlation r between the two half-profiles is computed with the same coefficient and genes as above. The Spearman–Brown formula, 2r/(1 + r), extrapolates this split-half reliability to the full set of cells under the assumption of equal halves, which the split guarantees *[REF]*. Its square root is the highest correlation any source can reach with that held-out profile. The split is repeated B times and the results are averaged *[B and seed to be fixed from the pilot]*. Types with few held-out cells have a lower ceiling, and fidelity is read against it.

### 2.6.2 Error of deconvolution

The primary estimand of the second aim is the proportion π_sk of cell type k in spot s. Unlike the scalar parameter θ of Morris et al. (2019), it is a vector per spot, and its error is summarised at two levels. Within replicate i, RMSE_i is the square root of the mean of (π̂_sk − π_sk)² over all spots and cell types, and JSD_i is the mean over spots of the Jensen–Shannon divergence between the estimated and true compositions. Across replicates, each measure is averaged over the n_sim replicates and reported with its Monte Carlo standard error (MCSE), SD(RMSE_i)/√n_sim, and likewise for JSD. Errors are computed for every combination of method, arm and condition, and on the anchor. RMSE is the primary measure and JSD the secondary one.

### 2.6.3 Rankings and their agreement

Within each arm and condition, the five methods are ranked by mean RMSE and, secondarily, by mean JSD. The agreement between the ranking of each arm and that of the anchor is measured with Kendall's τ, and the agreement among all arms with Kendall's W (PROTOCOLLO §5.5). Their MCSEs are obtained by bootstrap over replicates *[number of bootstrap samples to be fixed from the pilot]*. With five methods there are ten pairs, so τ takes eleven values and a single exchange between two methods lowers it from 1 to 0.8. For this reason the pairwise differences in RMSE between methods are also reported with their MCSEs, so that an exchange within Monte Carlo error is not read as a real change of ranking.

### 2.6.4 Decision rules

A ranking differs from the anchor, or from that of another arm, if at least one pairwise difference between two methods changes sign beyond twice its MCSE (DECISIONS 2026-10-30). The arm closest to the anchor is the one with the highest τ, with ties broken by the mean absolute difference in RMSE from the anchor *[pending Luca's approval]*.

### 2.6.5 Counter-proofs

Three checks are planned to falsify the results if they were wrong (PROTOCOLLO §5.6, as amended by DECISIONS 2026-10-26). First, the negative control C− must give low fidelity and rankings that differ from the anchor. Second, a synthetic source above E′ is not taken as evidence of leakage and is reported with its explanation: for M and G, the averaging over many donors described in Section 2.4.1; P, built de novo, has no such explanation, and a result of P above E′ is examined further. A source whose fidelity exceeds the noise ceiling beyond Monte Carlo error points to an error or to leakage and is investigated before the analysis continues. Third, leakage is checked on the provenance of the data in every case, not only when a source looks too good (DECISIONS 2026-10-26, leakage): an automatic test verifies that no donor appears in more than one split, and the generative arm is evaluated only on datasets absent from its training data (Section 2.2.3).

## 2.7 Number of replicates and Monte Carlo error

The number of replicates is chosen to reach a stated precision of the key performance measure, not fixed by convention. Earlier benchmarks of deconvolution fixed it without a precision target, for instance ten replicates in Spotless (Sang-aram et al. 2024) and five per setting in the simulations of CARD (Ma & Zhou 2022), while the comparison of simulators by Crowell et al. (2023) generated one dataset for each combination of reference and simulator (repository HelenaLC/simulation-comparison, version available at the time of writing). Following Morris et al. (2019), n_sim = (SD/target MCSE)², where SD is the standard deviation of the per-replicate error *[key measure, mean RMSE per method or between-method difference, and target MCSE pending Luca's approval]*. The SD is estimated in a pilot of ten replicates and is itself uncertain, with a relative standard error of about 1/√(2 · 9), or 24%. The upper 95% confidence limit of the SD is therefore used, which with nine degrees of freedom is about 1.83 times the estimate (DECISIONS 2026-10-30). The largest SD across the cells of the design is taken, so that every cell reaches the target; halving the MCSE requires four times as many replicates. The total cost, the product of replicates, data-generating mechanisms, methods and time per run, is checked against the available resources *[to be fixed from the pilot]*. cell2location, which runs on a shared GPU, may receive its own number of replicates, fixed by a dated amendment in the first days on the server and before any result (DECISIONS 2026-10-14).

The fidelity of E and E′ has no Monte Carlo error once the partition is fixed, since their μ is not random. MCSEs of fidelity are reported only where μ is random, in arms P, M, C− and G *[pending Luca's approval; departs from PROTOCOLLO §5.5, which asks for an MCSE for every measure]*.

The pilot that supplies these quantities is declared in advance, and its numbers do not enter the results (DECISIONS 2026-10-29, pilot, approved 2026-10-30). It runs in all three tissues, in the order colon, lymphoid tissue, brain, because the SD depends on the biology of each tissue and only a brain pilot can show whether the subsampling fallback of Section 2.3.1 is needed. It uses arms E and C− and the anchor, the four methods that run on a CPU (RCTD, SpatialDWLS, MuSiC and NNLS), and ten replicates for each arm and condition. The pilot never uses the held-out group: in each tissue a pilot held-out donor and a pilot comparator donor are drawn from the training split with a separate seed (20261030), and the pilot reference is built from the remaining training donors. The settings fixed by the pilot are therefore chosen within training, as with a validation split in machine learning, and the test donors are used once, in the main study. The pilot provides the SD of the error, the time per run, the types of failure and the seed check of Section 2.9.

## 2.8 Treatment of failed runs

No quality filter is applied to the pseudo-spots. The real cells are filtered upstream (Section 2.3.2), and adaptive filters designed to remove damaged cells and empty droplets have no target in spots built from filtered cells. Spots with few counts are the designed low-depth condition, and removing them would remove the hardest cases. Each method applies only its own built-in filter, as a user running the published pipeline would.

Following Morris et al. (2019, §5.1), failures are never discarded silently, because they tend to occur in the hardest datasets and removing them would make a method look better than it is. A failure is a spot that a method discards or cannot process, a run that does not converge, or an estimated composition whose proportions do not sum to one within a tolerance *[tolerance pending Luca's approval]*. Failures are counted for every combination of method, arm and condition and reported as a result. In the main analysis, methods are compared on the spots returned by all methods in the same replicate, so that every method is scored on the same spots. In a sensitivity analysis, failed spots receive the worst possible score, RMSE, τ and W are recomputed, and any change in the ranking is reported. Failures may still differ between arms through μ, as expected for C−; the types of failure that actually occur are identified in the pilot.

## 2.9 Software and reproducibility

All analyses are written in R *[version from `renv.lock`]*, except cell2location, which runs in a dedicated Python environment on the server *[environment and versions pending Luca's approval]*. The versions of all R packages are recorded in a lock file managed by renv, from which the environment is rebuilt on the server. Paths to the data are read from an environment variable and never written in the code, so the same scripts run unchanged on a personal computer and on the server (PROTOCOLLO §4.2).

The real data are obtained only through the script `01_download.R`, which downloads each dataset, computes its MD5 checksum and writes, or compares if it already exists, a manifest with the file, its URL, its size, its checksum and the date of download. The data are never placed under version control, only the manifest. On the server the script is run again and the checksums are compared with the manifest, which is the first check that the pipeline is reproducible; data are never copied from the personal computer. Downloaded files are read-only, and every derived file is written to a separate directory. Every call to GEM-1 is logged with its date, the model identifier, the full query and the MD5 checksum of the response, because the model may change over time and the log is the only way to reproduce arm G (PROTOCOLLO §9).

A single seed is fixed for the whole study *[seed set at approval]*, and the state of the random number generator is stored at the start of each replicate (Morris et al. 2019). Parallel processes use independent streams derived from that seed with the L'Ecuyer-CMRG generator. Because a package that resets the seed internally would make replicates correlated, the state of the generator is compared before and after two runs of each method in the pilot, and any such behaviour is corrected before the main study. The simulated datasets, or their seeds and generator states, are saved so that any replicate can be regenerated. Every figure and table is produced by a single command from the scripts and the data, and no file is edited by hand between one script and the next (PROTOCOLLO §3.5). Each stage of the work closes with a report of fixed structure, covering pre-registration, checks of software correctness, checks of biological plausibility and a counter-proof, and its key numbers are recomputed by a reviewer other than the author (PROTOCOLLO §3.4, §7). Any change to the protocol after approval is recorded as a dated amendment, written before the results it could affect, and the deviations are listed in an appendix.
